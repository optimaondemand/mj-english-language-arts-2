📖 TODAY'S FOCUS

The best twist endings are not tricks. They are promises the story kept all along. You just did not notice. Today you look at how 'The Most Dangerous Game' ends, what the twist reveals about irony, and then compare it with 'The Landlady' by Roald Dahl, which works with the same tool: what the writer does not say.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO...
1
Explain how the twist ending of 'The Most Dangerous Game' resolves the central irony.
2
Read 'The Landlady' on CommonLit and identify what Dahl leaves unsaid.
3
Annotate for irony: locate places in both stories where the reader knows something the character does not.

🔮 PART 1 · THE ANATOMY OF A TWIST
The Anatomy of a Twist
The Anatomy of a Twist
WHAT WE WERE NOT TOLD

The twist works because the writer withheld something. We had the clues. We just did not see them for what they were.

WHAT IRONY DOES AT THE END

Situational: the outcome flips what was expected. Dramatic: looking back, we see we knew all along. The twist is the moment both ironies land at once.

📖 PART 2 · THE MDG ENDING: WHAT CONNELL DID NOT SHOW
The Last Line
"He had never slept in a better bed, Rainsford decided."
Richard Connell, 'The Most Dangerous Game' (public domain)

This last line tells us Rainsford survived and won. But Connell does not show us the fight. We infer. The twist is the sudden resolution after all the drawn-out tension.

Looking back: the situational irony (the hunter becoming the hunted) is resolved. The dramatic irony (we always feared for Rainsford) pays off.

Annotation Guidance

As you read, watch for these three things. Annotate directly in CommonLit or in your Reading Journal.

ANNOTATE: WHAT DOES BILLY KNOW?

Moments where Billy feels inexplicably drawn or comfortable. Ask yourself: What does Billy know here? What does the reader suspect? Mark the gap.

ANNOTATE: FORESHADOWING OR DRAMATIC IRONY?

The small details: the pets, the register, the tea. For each one, ask: Is this foreshadowing (a hint at what comes) or dramatic irony (the reader understands something the character does not)? How does Dahl plant the clue?

ANNOTATE: WHAT IS LEFT UNSAID?

The ending. Ask: What does Dahl leave unsaid? How does silence work as a tool? Write one sentence about this in your Reading Journal.

🔍 TWO AUTHORS, ONE HIDDEN DANGER
Beyond the Twist: What Does Each Writer Seem to Believe?

So far you have looked at how Connell and Dahl build their twist endings through irony and withheld information. But both stories share more than a technique. They share a topic: danger that hides inside a person or place that seems ordinary, even welcoming. Each writer treats that idea differently. Neither one states it directly, so you have to work it out from what actually happens in the story.

Where Does Each Author Hide the Danger?
CONNELL: THE CIVILIZED HOST

General Zaroff is cultured, well mannered, and lives in a comfortable chateau. Rainsford, and the reader, first read him as a gracious host. Notice what the story suggests about danger that arrives dressed as refinement.

DAHL: THE ORDINARY LANDLADY

The landlady offers Billy exactly what a tired traveler wants: a cheap room, tea, a warm welcome. Notice what the story suggests about danger that arrives dressed as hospitality.

📝 READING JOURNAL

Both stories place their most dangerous character in a role we normally trust: a gracious host, a kind landlady. What does 'The Most Dangerous Game' seem to say about danger that hides behind good manners and comfort? What does 'The Landlady' seem to say about danger that hides behind ordinary kindness? Write your answer in your Reading Journal. Do this before moving on.

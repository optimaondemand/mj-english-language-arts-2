Lesson 1.01

The Hunter and the Hunted

~14 min
🎯 TODAY’S FOCUS
A hunter falls from a boat into the sea and swims toward an island with a dark reputation. What he finds there will turn his whole view of the world upside down. Today you read the opening of “The Most Dangerous Game” and discover how Richard Connell sets up the central irony of the story.
📋 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON, YOU WILL BE ABLE TO
  1. 1 Describe the story’s opening situation: what Rainsford knows and what he does not know.
  2. 2 Explain what situational irony means and identify it in the setup of “The Most Dangerous Game.”
  3. 3 Explain what dramatic irony means and describe how it works differently from situational irony.
🔮 WHAT YOU NEED
  • 📖  “The Most Dangerous Game” (PDF): [Teacher: link the MDG PDF in Canvas Files here]
  • 📝  Short Story Reading Journal (your Word doc on OneDrive)
💡 ONE BIG IDEA: TWO TYPES OF IRONY
Situational Irony
What happens is the OPPOSITE of what seems right or expected. In “The Most Dangerous Game”: Rainsford is an expert hunter. He has spent his whole career pursuing animals. Then he becomes the hunted animal himself. The very logic he believed in is turned against him.
Dramatic Irony
The READER knows something the character does not (yet). In “The Most Dangerous Game”: we sense that Zaroff hunts people before Rainsford fully admits it to himself. That gap between what we know and what Rainsford knows creates dread.

Both types of irony are at work in this story from the very first pages. Watch for them as you read.

☐ CHECK-IN: BEFORE YOU READ
A great hunter says that animals do not feel fear the way people do. Only the hunter feels the thrill. Do you agree? Pick the response that fits your thinking.
📖 READ & NOTICE: THREE MOMENTS TO WATCH FOR

Open the PDF now. Work through these three moments as you read. After each one, jot a brief note in your Reading Journal.

Moment 1  ·  Whitney’s Warning

Near the very beginning, Rainsford’s companion Whitney mentions Ship-Trap Island with unease. Rainsford is not troubled. Notice who senses danger and who dismisses it.

“The old charts call it Ship-Trap Island... It has a reputation, a bad one.”
📝 Jot in your Reading Journal: What does Whitney notice that Rainsford brushes aside? What does that tell you about Rainsford at this point in the story?
Moment 2  ·  Rainsford in the Water

Rainsford falls overboard. He hears screams from the direction of the island and swims toward it. He is now alone, in the dark, moving toward something he does not understand. The reader and the character are both in the dark, but watch what details Connell gives us.

“He struggled up to the surface and tried to cry out, but the wash from the speeding yacht slapped him in the face.”
📝 Jot in your Reading Journal: What is Rainsford thinking as he swims? What is the reader beginning to suspect?
Moment 3  ·  Meeting Zaroff

Rainsford reaches the island and eventually meets General Zaroff, a Cossack aristocrat who hunts. Zaroff describes his passion for the hunt and mentions needing “a new animal.” The reader grasps the meaning before Rainsford admits it to himself.

“I have no thrill left in tigers, no real danger.” / “I have invented a new sensation.”
📝 Jot in your Reading Journal: What do you understand about Zaroff’s “new sensation” before Rainsford does? This is the dramatic irony at work. What clues give it away?
☐ CHECK YOUR THINKING: WHICH TYPE OF IRONY?
Decide which type of irony fits each moment. Pick before you read the feedback.
Rainsford the expert hunter becomes Zaroff’s prey.
The reader suspects Zaroff hunts people while Rainsford still thinks he is just a remarkable collector.
Whitney warns that the island feels like “evil” and Rainsford dismisses it entirely.
📝 READING JOURNAL

Add this to the Week 1 section of your Reading Journal:

1. In your own words: how does Connell make the reader feel uneasy before Rainsford ever meets Zaroff? Point to one specific detail from the text.
2. Rainsford believes that “the world is made up of two classes, the hunters and the huntees.” By the end of Part 1, what has changed for him?

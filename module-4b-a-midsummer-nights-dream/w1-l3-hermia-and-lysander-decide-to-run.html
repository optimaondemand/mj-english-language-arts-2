Lesson 1.03
Hermia and Lysander Decide to Run
Lesson
⏱ ~13 min
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
One private decision is about to become everyone’s problem.
Facing the Duke’s ruling, Lysander and Hermia decide not to obey it. Today you read their plan to run away together and the very next moment, when a third character learns the secret and decides what to do with it. Watch how one choice sets off a chain of consequences that neither Hermia nor Lysander could control.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe the specific plan Lysander proposes to Hermia.
2
I will be able to explain why the couple chooses to run rather than accept the Duke’s ruling.
3
I will be able to identify the immediate consequence their decision sets off and explain why it happens.
📚 PART 1 · READ & NOTICE: WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
If a rule felt deeply unfair to you, would you be more likely to break it quietly or challenge it openly? (No wrong answer.)
📚 PART 2 · READ & NOTICE: THE PLAN

Once Hermia and Lysander are alone, Lysander proposes a way out.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 1, SCENE 1
I have a widow aunt, a dowager
Of great revenue, and she hath no child.
From Athens is her house remote seven leagues,
And she respects me as her only son.
There, gentle Hermia, may I marry thee,
And to that place the sharp Athenian law
Cannot pursue us. If thou lovest me then,
Steal forth thy father’s house tomorrow night;
And in the wood, a league without the town
(Where I did meet thee once with Helena
To do observance to a morn of May),
There will I stay for thee.
Lysander proposes a plan (Act 1, Scene 1)

Lysander’s aunt lives seven leagues (about twenty miles) outside Athens, far enough that “the sharp Athenian law” cannot reach them there. His plan has two parts: Hermia must slip out of her father’s house the following night, and the two of them will meet first in the wood outside the city before continuing on to the aunt’s house, where Athenian law has no power and they can marry freely.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE PLAN ACTUALLY SAY?
⚡ TRY IT
Answer each question about the plan using only what the text says. Decide in your head before you click.
1. Why does Lysander want to reach his aunt’s house specifically?
2. Where does Lysander first ask Hermia to meet him?
3. What kind of oath does Hermia swear to confirm she will meet Lysander?
📚 PART 3 · READ & NOTICE: THE CONSEQUENCE, ALMOST IMMEDIATELY

Helena, Hermia’s close friend, arrives just as the plan is being made. She loves Demetrius, the very man Egeus wants Hermia to marry, but Demetrius no longer wants Helena. After Hermia and Lysander tell her their secret and leave, Helena is alone on stage.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 1, SCENE 1
How happy some o’er other some can be!
Through Athens I am thought as fair as she.
But what of that? Demetrius thinks not so;
He will not know what all but he do know.
And as he errs, doting on Hermia’s eyes,
So I, admiring of his qualities.
Things base and vile, holding no quantity,
Love can transpose to form and dignity.
Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind;
And therefore is wing’d Cupid painted blind.
Nor hath love’s mind of any judgment taste.
Wings, and no eyes, figure unheedy haste.
And therefore is love said to be a child,
Because in choice he is so oft beguil’d.
As waggish boys in game themselves forswear,
So the boy Love is perjur’d everywhere.
For, ere Demetrius look’d on Hermia’s eyne,
He hail’d down oaths that he was only mine;
And when this hail some heat from Hermia felt,
So he dissolv’d, and showers of oaths did melt.
I will go tell him of fair Hermia’s flight.
Then to the wood will he tomorrow night
Pursue her; and for this intelligence
If I have thanks, it is a dear expense.
But herein mean I to enrich my pain,
To have his sight thither and back again.
Helena, alone on stage (Act 1, Scene 1)

Helena decides to tell Demetrius about the plan, hoping it will earn her a little of his gratitude, even though she already suspects it will cost her more than it gains her. One private decision, made by two people trying to escape an unfair law, is about to pull three more characters into the wood that same night.

✏️ READING JOURNAL · WEEK 1
In the Week 1 section of your Reading Journal, answer in 4 to 6 sentences: explain Lysander and Hermia’s plan in your own words, then predict what might go wrong once Demetrius and Helena both head into the same wood that night. Use at least one detail from today’s reading to support your prediction.

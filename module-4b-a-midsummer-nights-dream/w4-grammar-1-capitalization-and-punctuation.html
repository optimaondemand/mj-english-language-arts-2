📝 TODAY’S FOCUS
You will be able to apply the core rules of capitalization and end punctuation, and identify how they work inside a classified sentence.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you proofread your own writing, which rule do you find hardest to remember?
💡 THE CORE RULES AT A GLANCE
Capitalize
  • The first word of every sentence
  • The word I (always)
  • Proper nouns: specific people, places, organizations, days, and months (Puck, Athens, June)
  • Titles used directly before a name (Duke Theseus, Queen Titania)
Do NOT capitalize
  • Common nouns used generally (a fairy, the wood)
  • Seasons: spring, summer, fall, winter
  • Titles used alone or after a name (the duke; Theseus, a duke of Athens)
End punctuation: match it to the sentence’s purpose
  • Statement (declarative): period.
  • Question (interrogative): question mark?
  • Strong feeling (exclamatory): exclamation mark!
  • Command or request (imperative): period. (sometimes !)
📚 ANALYZING A SENTENCE
📚 THIS WEEK’S PRACTICE SENTENCE
“Puck, a mischievous fairy, moved quickly through the dark wood on a warm evening in June.”
  1. What is being said about Puck? He moved. The verb is moved (action verb).
  2. Who or what moved? Puck. Puck is the subject noun (a proper noun, since it names one specific character).
  3. What kind of word is fairy? A common noun. It renames Puck but does not get capitalized, because it is not a specific name.
  4. What proper noun names a specific month? June. Capitalized.
  5. What end mark fits this sentence? A period, because it is a statement.

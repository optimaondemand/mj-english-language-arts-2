💡 THE SHAPE OF A LITERARY ANALYSIS ESSAY
A literary analysis essay is not a summary of what happened. It is a claim about what the play shows, backed up with evidence from the text and an explanation of what that evidence means. The essay takes this shape, and each part grows from a Quick Write.
PART 1 · THESIS (from Quick Write 6)
Name whose account of the night in the wood is most believable, or explain why no single account fully earns trust. State what the play suggests about why people tell the stories they tell. One clear claim, defended through the whole essay.
PART 2 · EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION, FIRST PIECE (from Quick Writes 3 and 4)
Point to a specific moment where a character narrates an event to someone else. Cite it in MLA format: Shakespeare’s name plus act.scene. Then explain in original words what that moment shows about whose story deserves belief. The explanation is the part that does the real work.
PART 3 · EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION, SECOND PIECE (from Quick Writes 4 and 5)
Give a second moment, cited the same way. Explain how it adds to your case, and address one counterclaim: a reason someone might trust a different character’s account instead, and why the case still holds.
PART 4 · CONCLUSION (from Quick Write 6)
Restate your point in fresh words and say what the play suggests, overall, about why people tell the stories they tell. Do not just repeat the thesis sentence.
📜 CITING THE PLAY: AUTHOR, ACT.SCENE FORMAT

An essay about a play cites Shakespeare’s name plus act and scene, separated by a period, instead of a page number. This works the same way no matter which printed edition you use, because act and scene divisions stay the same across editions while page numbers do not.

How to read a citation: (Shakespeare, 1.1) means Shakespeare, Act 1, Scene 1. If Shakespeare is already named in the sentence, give only the act and scene: (1.1). When a short phrase is quoted directly, the citation goes right after the closing quotation mark, before the period that ends the sentence. A moment described in original words still takes the citation at the end of the sentence.

Example: Egeus asks Theseus for “the ancient privilege of Athens” to decide his daughter’s fate (Shakespeare, 1.1).

Use exactly this format for every piece of evidence, whether quoted directly or described in original words. At the end of the essay, add one Works Cited entry: Shakespeare, William. A Midsummer Night’s Dream.

📚 READ & NOTICE: A WORKED EXAMPLE

A short literary analysis of a different question about the play: whether the mechanicals’ performance of Pyramus and Thisbe succeeds or fails as storytelling. Each labeled part matches the shape above. The details are a model of the moves, not an answer to borrow.

THESIS The mechanicals’ telling of Pyramus and Thisbe fails as the story they intended, but it succeeds as a lesson in how badly a story can be told when the teller does not trust the audience to imagine anything on their own.

EVIDENCE 1 Snout, playing Wall, explains at length that he represents a wall and describes the chink in the wall rather than simply standing in as one (Shakespeare, 5.1). (Then the explanation:) This shows the mechanicals do not trust their audience to accept a simple symbol. Every detail is over-explained, which drains the story of the tension it needs.

EVIDENCE 2 Theseus and his court laugh and interrupt throughout the performance, at one point noting that “the best in this kind are but shadows” (Shakespeare, 5.1). (Then the explanation:) This shows that the audience’s own imagination has to complete a story; a poorly told tale asks too much of its listeners and gives them too little to work with.

CONCLUSION By making the mechanicals’ play fail so completely, the scene argues that a story only works when the teller trusts the listener to meet it partway.

Every piece of evidence is followed immediately by an explanation, and every citation uses author plus act.scene format. That combination is what earns credit, not the detail alone.

📚 CHECK THE THINKING

A student sentence, meant to be the explanation after a piece of evidence:

“This shows that Titania made up a story about the changeling boy’s mother because she loves the boy.”
⚡ TRY IT · NAME EACH PART OF THE ESSAY

These sentences from a literary analysis essay are out of order. Decide which part each one is: thesis, evidence, explanation, or conclusion.

1. “This reveals that Helena’s account of her friendship with Hermia is shaped by what she needs to argue in the moment, not by a neutral memory of the past.”
2. “Among all the accounts given at the end of the play, Bottom’s honest admission that he cannot explain his own vision is the most trustworthy, because he never pretends to understand more than he does.”
3. “In the end, the play suggests that people tell stories to make sense of what frightens or confuses them, and that a story’s honesty often shows in what it admits it cannot explain.”
4. “Egeus tells Theseus that Lysander has ‘bewitch’d the bosom’ of his daughter (Shakespeare, 1.1).”
🧠 BUILD THE PLAN

Fill in the four parts, with a personal position and personal citations:

Thesis: pull the claim from Quick Write 6. It names whose account is most believable, or explains why no account is fully trustworthy, and says something about why people tell stories.
Evidence 1 and its explanation: take the strongest moment from Quick Writes 3 or 4. Cite it in MLA format (Shakespeare’s name plus act.scene) and write one sentence that says what it shows about whose story to trust.
Evidence 2, its explanation, and a counterclaim: add a second cited moment from Quick Writes 4 or 5. Explain it, then name one reason someone might disagree with the case and say why the case still holds.
Conclusion: use the Quick Write 6 intro to restate the point in fresh words, then add one sentence about what the play suggests about storytelling as a whole.
💪 SUMMARY

The plan puts four Quick Writes into one clear shape: a thesis about whose story deserves belief, two cited pieces of evidence with real explanation, and a conclusion about why people tell stories.

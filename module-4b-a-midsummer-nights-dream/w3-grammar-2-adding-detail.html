Adding Detail: Adjectives, Adverbs, and Prepositional Phrases
Grammar
⏱ ~12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
A bare sentence gives the facts. A detailed sentence makes the reader see something. Today you will learn how to expand a sentence by adding adjectives, adverbs, and prepositional phrases.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (about Act 3)
Bare: “Titania loved him.”
Expanded: “The bewitched fairy queen loved him instantly, deep in the moonlit wood.”
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Read “Titania loved him” and then “The bewitched fairy queen loved him instantly, deep in the moonlit wood.” What does the second sentence do that the first does not? (No wrong answer.)
💡 THREE WAYS TO EXPAND A SENTENCE
Adjective
Describes a noun. Ask: What kind? Which one? How many?

“The bewitched queen loved him.”
Adverb
Describes a verb, adjective, or adverb. Ask: How? When? Where? To what extent?

“She loved him instantly.”
Prepositional Phrase
A preposition plus its object of the preposition. Adds location, time, or direction.

“...deep in the wood.”
📚 LABELING THE EXPANDED SENTENCE

Here is the full expanded sentence: “The bewitched fairy queen loved him instantly, deep in the moonlit wood.” Walk through it using the questions:

  1. Who or what loved? The queen. Queen is the subject noun.
  2. What is being said about the queen? She loved. That is the verb.
  3. The: what kind of adjective? An article adjective.
  4. What kind of queen? Bewitched, fairy. Both adjectives describing the subject noun.
  5. How did she love him? Instantly. That is an adverb describing the verb.
  6. Where? Deep in the moonlit wood. That is a prepositional phrase. The preposition is in; its object of the preposition is wood. The adjective moonlit describes that noun.

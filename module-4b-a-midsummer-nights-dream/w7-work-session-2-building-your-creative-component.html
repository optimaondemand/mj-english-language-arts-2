Work Session 2 · Project Week
Building Your Creative Component
Guided work session (ungraded)
~15 min
📖 WHAT THIS SESSION IS FOR
Show a story being told and judged, not just say it happens.
You will plan the creative half of your project. First, choose your creative option: a staging design, a character map of who believes what, or an illustrated scene. Then plan a VR scene that stages one moment where a character tells a story others must judge. Then write a short reflection that ties the whole thing together. By the end you will know exactly what to make for the MND Final Project - Creative + VR (assignment) page. This session is practice. It is not graded.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED OPEN
▶
Your Reading Journal, open to any notes on the narration spine: who tells a story to whom, and what the audience actually saw happen.
▶
Your VR headset and your choice of tools for the creative piece: a drawing or design app, or a Word doc for the character map.
▶
The MND Final Project - Creative + VR (assignment) page.
💡 YOUR THREE CREATIVE OPTIONS
Pick one. All three ask you to show something about the module question, not just describe the plot. Choose whichever fits how you think and work best.
OPTION A · STAGING DESIGN
Design the stage for one storytelling moment
Sketch or digitally design a stage set for one moment where a character narrates a story to others (Egeus to Theseus, Titania to Oberon, Helena to Hermia, Bottom to the mechanicals, or the lovers to Theseus and Hippolyta). Show where the teller stands, where the listeners stand, and one detail that hints at whether the story should be believed.
OPTION B · CHARACTER MAP
Map who believes what, and why
Build a visual map connecting each major storyteller to the people who hear their account, with a short note on whether that account is believed, doubted, or never really tested. Use lines, colors, or symbols to show the pattern across the whole play.
OPTION C · ILLUSTRATED SCENE
Illustrate one moment of telling
Illustrate one specific moment where a character tells their story (drawn, painted, or built digitally). Your illustration should capture the gap between what the character says happened and what the audience actually saw, so a viewer can sense the tension without being told about it directly.
All three options are equal in difficulty and graded on the same rubric. Choose the one that fits what you want to express.
🧠 PLAN YOUR VR SCENE

Your VR scene stages one moment where a character tells a story that others must judge. Put on your VR headset and choose any setting you like with enough open ground to arrange objects and labels.

⚠️ WHAT YOUR VR SCENE MUST DO
Pick one storytelling moment from the play. Build a space that shows a teller and a listener (or listeners), and use your own original labels to mark what the teller claims happened and what the listener actually knows or saw. You are not recreating the play’s costumes or exact staging; you are building a space that captures the act of telling and judging a story.
1
Choose your storytelling moment: Egeus’s formal plea, Titania’s tale about the changeling boy’s mother, Helena’s account of her friendship with Hermia, Bottom trying to describe his vision, or the lovers explaining the night to Theseus and Hippolyta.
2
Choose your setting freely. It does not need to look like Athens or the wood; any virtual environment with room to place two groups of objects or figures works.
3
Add two labels or sticky notes in your own words: one that states the teller’s claim, and one that states what the listener actually has reason to believe or doubt.
4
Record a short video or take a screenshot inside VR that shows your scene and both labels. If VR is not available to you, write a short description (about 100 words) of the scene you would build and why.
5
Add a spoken explanation, 30 to 60 seconds long: narrate what your scene shows and name the one point it makes about whether the story should be believed. Record this as part of your video, or as a short stand-alone clip if you captured a screenshot or wrote a description instead. This spoken part is required and graded for everyone.
📚 PLAN YOUR REFLECTION

Your reflection is 200 to 250 words. It connects your creative piece and your VR scene to the module question. It should explain what your choices show about whose stories deserve belief, not just describe what you made.

⚡ TRY IT · IS THIS REFLECTION SENTENCE STRONG OR WEAK?

For each sentence, decide: does it explain an idea about who should be believed and why (strong), only describe what was made (weak), or land somewhere in between? Decide first, then choose.

1. “My character map has six characters connected by lines to the people they tell stories to, with a color for believed and a color for doubted.”
2. “My map shows that the characters with more social power, like Theseus and Egeus, get believed automatically, while characters like Bottom and Helena have to work harder to be taken seriously.”
3. “In my staging design I put Bottom far from the mechanicals when he tells them about his dream, which felt right for the scene.”
4. “My two labels show that Egeus states his claim with total confidence while the label for Theseus only says what he actually knows, which is much less, and that gap is the whole reason the play makes his ruling feel unfair.”
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

You read the whole play, built your thinking across six Quick Writes, and now you are turning that thinking into something you made. Choose an option that lets you show who tells stories and who has to judge them. Build the VR scene around that same idea. Then let your reflection tie all three pieces together so a reader can see what you understood.

📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Good writers vary their sentence structure. Today you will learn the three main structures: simple, compound, and complex. You will practice naming each one and then build one of each yourself.

Simple: “Bottom rehearsed his lines in the wood.”
Compound: “Bottom rehearsed his lines in the wood, and Puck watched from the bushes.”
Complex: “Because Puck placed a donkey’s head on him, Bottom’s friends ran away in fear.”

💡 THE THREE SENTENCE STRUCTURES
Simple
One independent clause. One complete subject and predicate.

“Bottom rehearsed his lines in the wood.”
Compound
Two independent clauses joined by a conjunction (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so). Each half could stand alone.

“Bottom rehearsed, and Puck watched.”
Complex
One independent clause plus one dependent clause. The dependent clause starts with a subordinating conjunction (because, when, although, since, after, if).

“Because Puck tricked him, Bottom's friends fled.”
📚 HOW TO IDENTIFY THE STRUCTURE: ASK QUESTIONS ABOUT THE CLAUSES

Start with the compound sentence: “Bottom rehearsed his lines in the wood, and Puck watched from the bushes.”

  1. What is being said about Bottom? He rehearsed. Subject noun: Bottom. Verb: rehearsed. That is one complete clause.
  2. Is there another subject and verb after the conjunction "and"? Yes: Puck watched. Subject noun: Puck. Verb: watched. A second complete clause.
  3. Two independent clauses joined by a conjunction: this is a compound sentence.

Now the complex sentence: “Because Puck placed a donkey’s head on him, Bottom’s friends ran away in fear.”

  1. What is being said about Bottom's friends in the main part? They ran away. Subject noun: friends. Verb: ran. That part can stand alone.
  2. Can “Because Puck placed a donkey’s head on him” stand alone? No. It starts with the subordinating conjunction because, so it is a dependent clause.
  3. One independent clause plus one dependent clause: this is a complex sentence.

Semicolons, Colons, and Dashes: Marks That Do the Work of Words

📝 TODAY’S FOCUS
Some punctuation marks do the work of a whole word. Today you will learn when to use a semicolon, a colon, and a dash. Each one has a specific job, and choosing the right one changes how your sentence feels.
📚 THIS WEEK’S SENTENCE (original example)
“Egeus made one demand: Hermia must marry Demetrius; if she refused, the law offered only two paths -- death or a convent.”

💡 THE THREE MARKS AND THEIR JOBS
Semicolon  ;
Joins two complete, closely related sentences.
“The Duke ruled quickly; the court had no more questions.”
Colon  :
Introduces a list or explanation after a complete sentence.
“Hermia had three options: obey, marry, or refuse.”
Dash  (--)
Creates emphasis or a sharp, dramatic pause. Use sparingly.
“She had one path left -- her own choice.”
Note: in typed student writing, the long dash mark is written as two hyphens (--), which is how it appears in this lesson. Use it sparingly: one or two per page at most.
📚 HOW TO USE EACH MARK: ASK QUESTIONS

The surest way to choose the right mark is to ask questions about what each part of your sentence is doing. Watch how it works on this week’s sentence.

  1. What comes right after the first punctuation mark?  “Hermia must marry Demetrius,” a complete sentence explaining the demand. The colon signals that an explanation is coming after a complete statement.
  2. Could a period go there instead?  Yes, but the colon shows the reader that what follows explains exactly what the demand was, which is a tighter connection than a period allows.
  3. What joins the second and third parts?  A semicolon. “Hermia must marry Demetrius” and “if she refused, the law offered only two paths” can both stand as complete sentences on their own, and they are closely related, so the semicolon correctly links them.
  4. Where does the dash create emphasis?  Right before the final phrase, “death or a convent.” The dash sets that phrase apart and gives it a sharper, more dramatic weight than a comma would.

Weekly Workshop: Answering the Other Side
Lesson
⏱ ~12 min
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A strong argument does not ignore the other side. It states the other side fairly, then answers it.
On Day 1, Theseus and Hippolyta disagreed about the lovers' story. Today's short workshop teaches you a habit that will make your own writing stronger: before you argue for your own claim, state the opposing view honestly, in words a person who holds it would recognize as fair. Then answer it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to state an opposing view fairly, without weakening it on purpose.
2
I will be able to answer that opposing view with a specific reason of my own.
📚 PART 1 · RECALL DAY 1

On Day 1, Theseus argued that lovers, madmen, and poets all imagine things, so a story from any of them cannot be trusted. Hippolyta answered him directly.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 5, SCENE 1
But all the story of the night told over,
And all their minds transfigur’d so together,
More witnesseth than fancy’s images,
And grows to something of great constancy;
But, howsoever, strange and admirable.
Hippolyta, Act 5, Scene 1 (recall from Day 1)
📝 PART 2 · A WORKED EXAMPLE, DONE OPENLY

Here is what it looks like to state Theseus's side fairly and then answer it, using the same three moves every time.

MOVE 1: STATE THE OTHER SIDE FAIRLY
“Theseus might be right. Four lovers describing fairies, a love potion, and a man briefly wearing a donkey's head sound exactly like the kind of thing an overexcited imagination would invent, not something that actually happened.”
MOVE 2: EXPLAIN WHY SOMEONE COULD REASONABLY THINK THAT
“People's imaginations really can run wild under strong emotion, and Theseus has just given an entire speech about exactly that pattern.”
MOVE 3: ANSWER IT
“But Hippolyta's point still stands. The four lovers were not together when they first told their stories, yet their accounts agree on the same strange details. That kind of agreement is hard to explain away as four separate delusions.”
✎️ PART 3 · MAKE & APPLY

Choose one claim below, or use a claim of your own from earlier weeks.

  • The mechanicals' play is worth watching, even though it is badly performed.
  • Titania's love for the transformed Bottom is more sad than funny.
  • A claim of your own from this module's question, about whose account of the wood should be believed.
✏️ READING JOURNAL · WEEK 5
Using your chosen claim, write your own three moves: state the opposing view fairly, explain why someone could reasonably hold it, then answer it. Three to four sentences total. Add this to the Week 5 section of your Reading Journal.

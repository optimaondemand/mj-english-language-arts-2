💡 THE ONE RULE TO REMEMBER: SHORT GETS QUOTES, LONG GETS ITALICS
Short works: quotation marks
  • A ballad: “Bottom’s Dream” (the one Bottom asks Peter Quince to write)
  • Short stories and poems
  • Song titles
  • A single scene or chapter title
Long works: italics (or underline in handwriting)
  • Plays: A Midsummer Night’s Dream
  • Novels and full-length films
  • Albums
  • Newspapers and magazines
Put the two together, exactly as Bottom would say it: he wants Peter Quince to write a ballad called “Bottom’s Dream” (short work, quotation marks) to be sung inside the play A Midsummer Night’s Dream (long work, italics).
📚 QUOTING A LINE OF DIALOGUE: THE RULES

When you quote exact words a character says, place them inside quotation marks. A signal phrase (words like Bottom announced or Titania asks) is followed by a comma, then the opening quotation mark. In American English, the period or comma at the end goes inside the closing quotation mark.

📚 PRACTICE SENTENCE
Bottom told his friends, “I am to discourse wonders.”
  1. What is being said about Bottom? He told. The verb is told (action verb).
  2. Who or what told? Bottom. Bottom is the subject noun (proper noun, capitalized).
  3. What kind of word is his? A possessive pronoun: it shows the friends belong to Bottom.
  4. Where does the period go? Inside the closing quotation mark, since it is American English convention.
Classified sentence:
Bottom
subject noun (proper)told
verb (action)his
possessive pronounfriends,
noun“...”
quotation, period inside

One more rule: a colon can introduce a longer or more formal quotation, but a comma is standard after a short signal phrase like told, said, or announced.

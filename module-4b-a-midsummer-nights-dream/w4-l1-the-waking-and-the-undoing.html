The Waking and the Undoing

🎯 TODAY’S FOCUS
By the end of the night in the wood, Oberon has undone the spell on Titania and ordered the ass’s head removed from Bottom. Then Duke Theseus finds the four young lovers asleep. Each one wakes up believing something different about what happened. You will read what each character says, and start deciding whose account you trust.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a time you woke up from a strange dream. For a moment, how sure were you about what was real?
📚 READ & NOTICE: UNDOING THE SPELL

Oberon has what he wanted (the changeling boy), so he decides to end the trick. He touches Titania’s eyes with a different flower and orders Puck to take the donkey’s head off Bottom. Titania wakes with no memory of choosing an ordinary weaver to love. She only knows the feeling is suddenly, strangely gone.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 4, SCENE 1
Be as thou wast wont to be;
See as thou was wont to see.
Dian’s bud o’er Cupid’s flower
Hath such force and blessed power.
Now, my Titania, wake you, my sweet queen.

TITANIA.
My Oberon, what visions have I seen!
Methought I was enamour’d of an ass.
Oberon undoes the enchantment on Titania and orders Puck to remove the ass’s head from Bottom.

Notice Titania’s reaction: she is confused and a little embarrassed once the spell lifts, and the play plays this for comedy rather than romance. Her memory of the night has already started to feel unreal to her, the same thing that is about to happen to the four young Athenians.

📚 READ & NOTICE: FOUND IN THE WOOD

Duke Theseus is out hunting with Hippolyta and Egeus when they come across Lysander, Demetrius, Hermia, and Helena asleep on the ground. He has them woken with hunting horns. What each one says next is the heart of this lesson: everyone has to explain, out loud, in front of the Duke, how they got there and what they now feel.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 4, SCENE 1
LYSANDER.
My lord, I shall reply amazedly,
Half sleep, half waking; but as yet, I swear,
I cannot truly say how I came here.
But, as I think (for truly would I speak)
And now I do bethink me, so it is:
I came with Hermia hither. Our intent
Was to be gone from Athens, where we might be
Without the peril of the Athenian law.

EGEUS.
Enough, enough, my lord; you have enough.
I beg the law, the law upon his head.
They would have stol’n away, they would, Demetrius,
Thereby to have defeated you and me:
You of your wife, and me of my consent,
Of my consent that she should be your wife.

DEMETRIUS.
My lord, fair Helen told me of their stealth,
Of this their purpose hither to this wood;
And I in fury hither follow’d them,
Fair Helena in fancy following me.
But, my good lord, I wot not by what power,
(But by some power it is) my love to Hermia,
Melted as the snow, seems to me now
As the remembrance of an idle gaud
Which in my childhood I did dote upon;
And all the faith, the virtue of my heart,
The object and the pleasure of mine eye,
Is only Helena. To her, my lord,
Was I betroth’d ere I saw Hermia.
But like a sickness did I loathe this food.
But, as in health, come to my natural taste,
Now I do wish it, love it, long for it,
And will for evermore be true to it.

THESEUS.
Fair lovers, you are fortunately met.
Of this discourse we more will hear anon.
Egeus, I will overbear your will;
For in the temple, by and by with us,
These couples shall eternally be knit.
And, for the morning now is something worn,
Our purpos’d hunting shall be set aside.
Away with us to Athens. Three and three,
We’ll hold a feast in great solemnity.
Come, Hippolyta.
Duke Theseus finds the four lovers asleep in the wood. Lysander, Egeus, and Demetrius each give the Duke a different account of the night.

Lysander admits he is amazedly confused (astonished, still half caught between sleep and waking) and cannot fully explain how he got there. Egeus, still angry, demands the old law be enforced against Lysander. But Demetrius’s account changes everything: his love for Hermia has “melted as the snow,” and his love for Helena, the woman he was betrothed to before any of this began, has come back and feels permanent. Theseus overbears Egeus’s objection (he overrules it, using his greater authority) and allows all three couples to marry.

⚡ CHECK YOUR THINKING
⚡ WHO BELIEVES WHAT
Read each question and choose the answer that best matches what the text says.
1. What does Lysander tell Duke Theseus about how he ended up in the wood?
2. What does Demetrius say happened to his feelings for Hermia?
3. When the four lovers are left alone, what do they agree on?
📚 READ & NOTICE: ARE WE AWAKE?
📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 4, SCENE 1
DEMETRIUS.
These things seem small and undistinguishable,
Like far-off mountains turnèd into clouds.

HERMIA.
Methinks I see these things with parted eye,
When everything seems double.

HELENA.
So methinks.
And I have found Demetrius like a jewel,
Mine own, and not mine own.

DEMETRIUS.
Are you sure
That we are awake? It seems to me
That yet we sleep, we dream. Do not you think
The Duke was here, and bid us follow him?

HERMIA.
Yea, and my father.

HELENA.
And Hippolyta.

LYSANDER.
And he did bid us follow to the temple.

DEMETRIUS.
Why, then, we are awake: let’s follow him,
And by the way let us recount our dreams.
Left alone, the four lovers compare what they remember. None of them is fully certain they are awake.

Helena says she has found Demetrius “like a jewel, mine own, and not mine own,” a way of saying something can feel both real and unreal at once. The audience knows exactly what happened in the wood: Puck’s flower juice, the mistaken charm on Lysander, Oberon’s fix. The four lovers know none of that. They only have their own confused memory to go on, which is exactly this module’s question: whose account can we trust, and how would we know?

💡 FOUR ACCOUNTS, ONE NIGHT
Lysander
Honestly uncertain. Admits he cannot fully explain how he got there.
Demetrius
Certain, but his certainty is new. His love changed overnight and he cannot fully explain why either.
Hermia & Helena
Relieved but still doubting. They describe seeing things “with parted eye,” doubled and unclear.
The audience
The only one who saw everything: the flower, the mistake, the fix. The audience knows more than any character in the wood.

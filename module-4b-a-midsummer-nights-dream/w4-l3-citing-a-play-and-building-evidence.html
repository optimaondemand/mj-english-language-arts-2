Citing a Play and Building Evidence

🎯 TODAY’S FOCUS
A play is not a novel. It has no chapters and no page a reader can point to the same way every time. Today you learn the format writers use to quote and cite a play exactly: the playwright’s name, plus act and scene. You will use this exact format in your final project essay.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you cite a novel, you usually give the author’s last name and a chapter or page number. What do you think you would cite for a play written mostly in verse?
💡 THE FORMAT: AUTHOR, ACT.SCENE
Shakespeare
Author
The playwright’s last name. Always comes first.
4
Act
The large division of the play. This module reads Act 4.
1
Scene
The smaller division inside the act. Act 4 has two scenes.
Put together: (Shakespeare, 4.1) means Shakespeare, Act 4, Scene 1.
Our pages carry no page numbers, so act and scene are what you cite: the two divisions any reader can find no matter which printed copy they check. If you already name Shakespeare in your own sentence, do not name him again in the parentheses; just give the act and scene.
📚 QUOTING FEWER THAN THREE LINES OF VERSE

When you quote one or two lines of verse, you can run the quotation right into your own sentence. Mark where one line of the original ends and the next begins with a forward slash, with a space on each side: / .

Worked example:

Titania’s shock is immediate: “My Oberon, what visions have I seen! / Methought I was enamour’d of an ass.” (Shakespeare, 4.1).

Notice the slash stands in for the line break that the block quote below shows visually. The period comes after the closing parenthesis, since the citation ends the sentence.

📚 QUOTING THREE OR MORE LINES OF VERSE

Once you are quoting three lines or more, set the quotation off from your paragraph as its own indented block. Drop the quotation marks (the indenting shows the reader it is a quotation) and keep the original line breaks exactly as printed.

Worked example: Oberon’s spell that restores Titania’s sight
Be as thou wast wont to be;
See as thou was wont to see.
Dian’s bud o’er Cupid’s flower
Hath such force and blessed power.
(Shakespeare, 4.1)

Because the citation is set on its own line after a block quotation, no extra period follows it. The period from the end of the quoted material already does that work.

📚 QUOTING PROSE

Not every character speaks in verse. Bottom often speaks in prose, and the mechanicals always do. Prose is quoted like ordinary prose: no slash marks are needed, since prose is not built from fixed lines the way verse is. The citation still gives Shakespeare’s name plus act and scene.

Worked example:

Bottom insists, “I have had a most rare vision. I have had a dream, past the wit of man to say what dream it was.” (Shakespeare, 4.1).

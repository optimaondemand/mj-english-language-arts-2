Lesson 3.02
Bottom Transformed and Titania in Love
Comic Confusion in the Wood
⏱ ~15 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
While the workmen rehearse, Puck plays a trick on Bottom: he gives him the head of a donkey. Bottom has no idea anything has changed. At the same moment, Titania wakes under the flower’s spell and falls in love with the first thing she sees, which is now Bottom, donkey head and all. Today you will look at how the wood keeps changing people, and at what the audience knows that the characters do not.

📚 FROM THE PLAY

Puck has already given Bottom a donkey’s head while he was offstage. Read what happens when Bottom returns to his rehearsal.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 3, SCENE 1
O Bottom, thou art changed! What do I see on thee?

BOTTOM.
What do you see? You see an ass-head of your own, do you?

[_Exit Snout._]

Enter Quince.

QUINCE.
Bless thee, Bottom! bless thee! Thou art translated.

[_Exit._]

BOTTOM.
I see their knavery. This is to make an ass of me, to fright me, if
they could. But I will not stir from this place, do what they can. I
will walk up and down here, and I will sing, that they shall hear I am
not afraid.
[_Sings._]
The ousel cock, so black of hue,
With orange-tawny bill,
The throstle with his note so true,
The wren with little quill.

TITANIA.
[_Waking._] What angel wakes me from my flowery bed?

BOTTOM.
[_Sings._]
The finch, the sparrow, and the lark,
The plain-song cuckoo gray,
Whose note full many a man doth mark,
And dares not answer nay.
for, indeed, who would set his wit to so foolish a bird? Who would give
a bird the lie, though he cry ‘cuckoo’ never so?

TITANIA.
I pray thee, gentle mortal, sing again.
Mine ear is much enamour’d of thy note.
So is mine eye enthrallèd to thy shape;
And thy fair virtue’s force perforce doth move me,
On the first view, to say, to swear, I love thee.
Snout sees Bottom's new head and flees. Quince confirms it: "Thou art translated." Bottom, unaware, sings to prove he is not afraid, and Titania wakes under the flower's spell.
📚 WHAT THE AUDIENCE KNOWS THAT BOTTOM DOES NOT

This is called dramatic irony: the audience can see something a character cannot. We see the donkey head. Bottom cannot. When his friends scream and run, Bottom thinks they are the ones acting strange: “This is a knavery of them to make me afeard.” Knavery means a dishonest trick. Bottom believes he is the victim of a prank, when really the trick has already happened to him, and he has no idea.

Titania is under a spell too. The flower’s juice makes her fall instantly in love with the first creature she sees. The play does not want you to take this as real love. It is a magical mistake, meant to look strange and a little ridiculous, not romantic. Titania, a powerful fairy queen, adoring a man with a donkey’s head is the joke, and the play knows it.

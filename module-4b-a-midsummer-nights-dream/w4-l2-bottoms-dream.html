🎯 TODAY’S FOCUS
Bottom wakes up alone with his own head back, no memory of a donkey’s ears, only a feeling that something enormous happened to him. He tries to describe it and cannot. Today you look at what he does instead, and what that choice tells you about stories that will not fit into words.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Have you ever tried to describe something and found that plain words were not enough? What did you do instead?
💡 STORYTELLERS SO FAR
This play has no narrator, so its characters keep telling each other stories instead. Egeus told Duke Theseus a slanted story about Lysander “stealing” his daughter’s heart. Titania told Oberon a tender story about a dead friend’s son. Helena told Hermia a story about their childhood friendship, using it to argue for loyalty in the present. Last week you watched four lovers try to tell the Duke, and each other, what happened to them overnight. Now it is Bottom’s turn, and his story is the strangest one yet.
📚 READ & NOTICE: A MOST RARE VISION

Bottom wakes up mid-sentence, still thinking he is about to rehearse his part in the mechanicals’ play. Slowly he realizes his friends are gone. Then he tries to put his night into words.

📜 FROM THE PLAY · ACT 4, SCENE 1
BOTTOM.
[_Waking._] When my cue comes, call me, and I will answer. My next is
‘Most fair Pyramus.’ Heigh-ho! Peter Quince! Flute, the bellows-mender!
Snout, the tinker! Starveling! God’s my life! Stol’n hence, and left me
asleep! I have had a most rare vision. I have had a dream, past the wit
of man to say what dream it was. Man is but an ass if he go about to
expound this dream. Methought I was—there is no man can tell what.
Methought I was, and methought I had—but man is but a patched fool if
he will offer to say what methought I had. The eye of man hath not
heard, the ear of man hath not seen, man’s hand is not able to taste,
his tongue to conceive, nor his heart to report, what my dream was. I
will get Peter Quince to write a ballad of this dream: it shall be
called ‘Bottom’s Dream’, because it hath no bottom; and I will sing it
in the latter end of a play, before the Duke. Peradventure, to make it
the more gracious, I shall sing it at her death.
Bottom wakes alone in the wood. His friends have already fled, and his head is his own again, though he does not know that yet.

Notice what Bottom actually says: over and over, he tells you what his dream is not. No eye has heard it, no ear has seen it, no hand can taste it, no tongue can conceive it, no heart can report it. He lists every sense in the wrong order on purpose, which is its own kind of joke: he is so overwhelmed that even his description of being overwhelmed comes out scrambled. He never once tells you what actually happened (the donkey’s head, the fairy queen’s attention). He only tells you that it cannot be told.

⚡ CHECK YOUR THINKING
⚡ A STORY ITS OWN TELLER CANNOT TELL
Answer each question using what Bottom actually says and does.
1. Why does Bottom say he cannot describe his dream?
2. What does Bottom decide to do about a dream he cannot describe?
3. Compare what you, the audience, know to what Bottom knows. What is the difference?
✏️ READING JOURNAL · WEEK 4
In the Week 4 section of your Reading Journal, explain in your own words why Bottom cannot describe his dream, and what he decides to do about it instead. Then add a sentence comparing Bottom’s situation to one of the four lovers from Lesson 4.01: who has more trouble putting their night into words, and why?

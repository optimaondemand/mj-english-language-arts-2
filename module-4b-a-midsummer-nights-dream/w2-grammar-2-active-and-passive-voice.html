Active and Passive Voice

TODAY’S FOCUS
~12 min
A sentence in active voice has the subject doing the action. A sentence in passive voice has the subject receiving the action. Both are correct, but they do different things for a reader. Today you will learn to tell them apart and choose between them.
ACTIVE VS. PASSIVE VOICE
ACTIVE VOICE
The subject acts.
  • The subject noun performs the action.
  • The verb is a direct action verb.
  • Usually clearer and more direct.
EXAMPLE
“Oberon overheard the lovers’ quarrel.”
Subject (Oberon) performs the action.
→
TRANSFORM
←
PASSIVE VOICE
The subject is acted on.
  • The subject receives the action.
  • The verb includes a helping verb (is, was, were, been, etc.) plus a past participle.
  • Can emphasize what happened rather than who did it.
EXAMPLE
“The lovers’ quarrel was overheard by Oberon.”
Subject (the quarrel) receives the action.
WARM-UP
Which sounds more direct to you: “Puck mixed up the two men” or “the two men were mixed up by Puck”? Pick one and say why. (No wrong answer.)
👁 READ & NOTICE
CLASSIFYING THE TWO FOCUS SENTENCES
ACTIVE VOICE SENTENCE
“Oberon overheard the lovers’ quarrel.”

Step 1 What is being said about Oberon? Oberon overheard. Overheard is the verb (action verb).

Step 2 Who or what overheard? Oberon. Oberon is the subject noun.

Step 3 Oberon is doing the action. This is active voice.

PASSIVE VOICE SENTENCE
“The lovers’ quarrel was overheard by Oberon.”

Step 1 What is being said about the quarrel? The quarrel was overheard. Was overheard is the verb (helping verb plus main verb).

Step 2 Who or what was overheard? The quarrel. Quarrel is the subject noun. Notice: it is not doing the overhearing; it is receiving the action.

Step 3 Oberon appears after “by” and is the agent. Quarrel is the subject noun. This is passive voice.

KEY RULE
To spot passive voice: look for a helping verb (was, were, is, are, been, being) paired with a past-participle main verb. If the doer of the action appears at all, it will usually follow “by.”
📝 PRACTICE
ACTIVE OR PASSIVE?
Read each sentence. Decide whether it uses active or passive voice.
“Titania told the story of the changeling boy’s mother.” Active or passive voice?
“The wood was filled with fairies and moonlight.” Active or passive voice?
“The mischievous sprite mixed up the two Athenian men.” Active or passive voice?
“The wrong lover’s eyes were touched with the magic juice.” Active or passive voice?
WHEN TO USE WHICH
  • Use active voice when you want to be direct and clear about who is doing what.
  • Use passive voice when what happened matters more than who did it, or when the doer is unknown.
  • In analytical writing about the play, active voice is usually the stronger choice.

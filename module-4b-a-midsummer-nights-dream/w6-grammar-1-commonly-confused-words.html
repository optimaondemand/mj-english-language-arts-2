Words That Sound the Same, Mean Different Things
Lesson

📝 TODAY’S FOCUS
⌚ ~12 min
Some word pairs sound almost the same but do different jobs in a sentence. A spellchecker often misses them, because each word is spelled correctly on its own. Today you will learn four pairs and a quick test for telling them apart.
📝 A SENTENCE TO WATCH
“Their account of the night felt believable, but you’re the one who decides whether it’s true.”
Every confused-word pair below is used correctly in this sentence.
📚 FOUR PAIRS, ONE RULE EACH
its / it’s
its is a possessive pronoun (belonging to it): “The play lost its ending.”
it’s is a contraction of “it is” or “it has”: “It’s a strange story.”
their / there / they’re
their is a possessive pronoun: “their account.”
there is a place word: “the fairies met there.”
they’re is a contraction of “they are”: “they’re certain of it.”
your / you’re
your is a possessive pronoun: “your outline.”
you’re is a contraction of “you are”: “you’re the judge of that.”
then / than
then is a time word: “first the lovers speak, then Theseus answers.”
than is a comparing word: “more believable than the mechanicals’ play.”
A fast test for the contractions: say the sentence with “it is,” “they are,” or “you are” instead. If it still makes sense, the contraction is correct.

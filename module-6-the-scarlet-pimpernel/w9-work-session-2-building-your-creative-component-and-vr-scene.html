Building Your Creative Component and VR Scene
Guided work session (ungraded)
~15 min
📖 WHAT THIS SESSION IS FOR
Show the ideas of heroism and disguise. Do not just describe them.
You will plan and begin the creative half of your project: a visual component in a format you choose, a platform-agnostic VR thematic scene staging heroism or disguise from The Scarlet Pimpernel, and a short reflection that connects what you made to the novel’s argument. By the end you will know exactly what to build and submit on the Scarlet Pimpernel Final Project - Creative + VR (assignment) page. This session is practice. It is not graded.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED OPEN
▶
Any notes you made on heroism, disguise, loyalty, and courage across Weeks 1 through 7. These are your source of ideas.
▶
Your VR headset and whatever you will use for the visual: paper and supplies, a drawing or design app, or a document.
▶
The Scarlet Pimpernel Final Project - Creative + VR (assignment) page open.
💡 THE TWO IDEAS AT THE HEART OF YOUR PROJECT
Your creative work and VR scene should put one of two ideas at the center. Choose the one that your essay argues about, so both components are working on the same question.
THEME OPTION A
Heroism in the open
Some acts of courage are visible: a rescue at speed, a stand taken in public, a risk everyone can see. Your creative work could stage a moment where that visible heroism is on display and ask what it costs.
⇆
THEME OPTION B
Heroism in disguise
Other acts of courage hide behind a mask: the hero who acts precisely because no one suspects him. Your creative work could stage the tension between what the world sees and what is actually happening beneath the surface.
Pick one theme and stay with it across your creative component, your VR scene, and your reflection. A focused project is stronger than one that tries to show everything at once.
📚 STEP 1 · CHOOSE YOUR CREATIVE FORMAT

Pick one format for your visual component. Whatever you choose, it should make your selected theme visible without just illustrating a plot summary. The question it should answer is: what does this moment show about heroism or disguise?

Format What it is Strong if you want to show
Illustrated sceneOne or two panels showing a key moment, hand-drawn or digital, with a title or caption that names the theme.contrast between appearance and reality (what others see vs. what is true).
Symbol arrangementA visual using objects or symbols from the novel (the red flower, a mask, a letter, a scarlet scarf) arranged to represent the theme, photographed or drawn.the ideas beneath the plot without narrating specific scenes.
Comic stripThree to five panels telling a short sequence: what others see, what is actually happening, and what it reveals about heroism or disguise.the gap between public appearance and secret action in a story form.
Character profileA split portrait of Sir Percy: one side showing what the world sees (the fop), the other showing what he actually is (the Pimpernel), with labels naming the qualities of each.how disguise works as a character strategy rather than just a plot device.
📚 STEP 3 · PLAN YOUR REFLECTION

Your reflection is 200 to 250 words. It should explain what your creative component and VR scene show about heroism or disguise in the novel, not just describe what you made. Here is exactly what a strong reflection does.

WHAT A STRONG REFLECTION DOES (read this before you write)
1.
It names the idea: it says what your creative work shows about heroism or disguise, not just what objects or images are in it. Move from describing to explaining.
2.
It points to at least one specific choice you made (a label you wrote, an object you placed, a contrast you staged) and explains why you made that choice.
3.
It connects what you made to Orczy’s argument in the novel. Your creative work should extend your essay’s claim into a different form. Say that in the reflection: how do your visual and VR scene support or complicate your essay’s answer to the module question?

In your notebook, draft two or three sentences that do each of the three things above before you type the full reflection. Use those sentences as the skeleton of your 200 to 250 word piece.

⚡ TRY IT · IS THIS REFLECTION SENTENCE STRONG OR WEAK?

For each sentence, decide whether it explains an idea about heroism or disguise (strong), only describes what is in the scene (weak), or is somewhere in between. Decide first, then choose.

1. “On the left side of my scene I put a red flower and a mask, and on the right side I put an open road with a label.”
2. “I placed the mask closest to the viewer so that the first thing anyone sees is the disguise, not the hero behind it, which is the same position Orczy puts the reader in at the start of the novel.”
3. “I wrote the label ‘What courage costs’ because I wanted viewers to think about sacrifice, which felt important to my theme.”
4. “My VR scene extends my essay’s claim by showing that heroism in disguise requires two simultaneous performances: one for the audience that must not suspect, and one for the cause that must succeed, and Orczy argues that both take equal courage.”
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

You have argued in writing that courage in this novel works in a particular way. Now you make something that shows it. Choose one theme, build a visual and a VR scene that makes that theme feel real to a viewer, and then write a reflection that says why you made the choices you made. The goal is not a beautiful picture. The goal is a picture that means something.

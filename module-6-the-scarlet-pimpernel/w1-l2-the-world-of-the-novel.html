📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Chapter 1 shows you revolutionary Paris. Chapter 2 cuts to England, to a warm inn at Dover, where ordinary English people discuss the chaos across the water. Two worlds, one novel: what holds them together?
Today you will map the novel’s two-world structure and explore how Orczy uses setting to build contrast, tension, and the stakes of the Pimpernel’s mission.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

I will be able to describe how Orczy contrasts Paris and Dover in Chapters 1 and 2.
2
I will be able to explain what the Day Dream (the Pimpernel’s yacht) represents in the novel’s escape machinery.
3
I will be able to identify how Orczy uses setting to create mood and contrast.

💡 THE NOVEL'S TWO WORLDS
PARIS (Ch. 1)
The guillotine runs all day.
Barricades with armed guards.
Crowds watching executions.
Nobles fleeing in disguise.
Fear, violence, suspicion.
↔
DOVER (Ch. 2)
A warm inn, the Fisherman’s Rest.
Pewter mugs, firelight, laughter.
English farmers debating politics.
Sally serving beer, a stranger toasting.
Safety, comfort, good humor.
The contrast is the point: Orczy wants you to feel what safety looks like, so the danger across the Channel feels real.
📚 PART 1 · READ & NOTICE: CHAPTER 2
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Chapter 2 opens in a busy English inn, completely different from the barricade of Chapter 1. What effect does that shift have on you as a reader?

Read Chapter 2 now. You enter the Fisherman’s Rest in Dover, where landlord Mr. Jellyband, his daughter Sally, and regulars like Mr. Hempseed discuss the wet September weather and the chaos in France. A well-dressed stranger and his companion are also at the inn, listening quietly and, it seems, finding Mr. Jellyband’s bluster quietly amusing. Keep an eye on that stranger.

✏️ READING JOURNAL · WEEK 1
In the Week 1 section of your Reading Journal, answer in 3 to 4 sentences: How does the Fisherman’s Rest in Chapter 2 feel different from the barricade in Chapter 1? Name two specific details from each chapter that show the contrast. What does the contrast tell you about the stakes of the Pimpernel’s work?
📚 PART 2 · READ & NOTICE: SETTING DETAILS THAT DO WORK

Orczy uses specific, concrete details to build each setting. Below are four details from Chapter 2. For each one, think about what the detail shows before you click to see what a careful reader might notice. The cards flip: read the front, make your own observation, then click.

Detail from Ch. 2
“Pewter on the fine old dressers shone like silver and gold.”
What does this show? Click to check.
The inn is well-kept and prosperous. Jellyband takes pride in it. This is a place of order and comfort, the opposite of the barricade.
Detail from Ch. 2
A stranger with “a quiet, sarcastic smile” listens to Jellyband’s opinions.
What might this hint at? Click to check.
The stranger knows more than he is letting on. His quiet smile suggests he sees something Jellyband does not. This is the kind of hint Orczy plants before a big reveal.
Detail from Ch. 2
Jellyband mentions that Lord Tony and Sir Andrew Ffoulkes are expected with “friends from over the water.”
Who are these friends? Click to check.
These are nobles who have already been rescued and smuggled to England. The inn is the safe landing point. The rescue route runs from Paris, across the Channel, and ends here at Dover.
Detail from Ch. 2
Jellyband boasts he could never be tricked by a Frenchman, since he would spot a foreign accent immediately.
Why might Orczy include this boast? Click to check.
Orczy is setting up dramatic irony: the reader will eventually know more than Jellyband about who is really in the room. His confidence makes the joke on him all the richer.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND LITERARY DETAILS
An AI tool can instantly summarize what happens in Chapter 2, but it cannot feel the shift in mood between Paris and Dover the way you do when you read both chapters in order. That feeling, and what you make of it, is your reading. Summaries skip the experience that builds your judgment.

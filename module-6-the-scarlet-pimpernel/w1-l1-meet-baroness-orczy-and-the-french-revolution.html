📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Paris, 1792. A crowd watches the guillotine. Somewhere in the city, an unknown Englishman is helping condemned nobles escape. Who is he, and why does he keep risking his life for people he has never met?
Today you meet Baroness Orczy and the world she created: France in the grip of the Reign of Terror, the estate system that drove the revolution, and a mysterious hero known only by a small red flower.

💡 THE ONE IDEA TO HOLD ONTO
France before the Revolution was divided into three estates. The estates determined who had power and who carried the burden. Understanding them explains the rage Orczy puts on every page of Chapter 1.
☺
First Estate
The clergy. Exempt from most taxes. Owned about 10% of French land.
+
🌟
Second Estate
The nobility. Held privileges, titles, and vast land. Orczy’s aristos belong here.
+
👨
Third Estate
Everyone else: merchants, farmers, the poor. Paid heavy taxes. About 98% of the population.
By 1789 the Third Estate had had enough. The French Revolution began. By 1792 (where Chapter 1 opens), the guillotine was running daily and the nobility was fleeing for their lives.

📚 PART 2 · READ & NOTICE: BARONESS ORCZY AND THE NOVEL

A Hungarian-British author who invented the secret hero

Emma Orczy was born in 1865 in Hungary and eventually settled in England. She published The Scarlet Pimpernel in 1905, over a century after the events it describes. The novel grew out of a play she co-wrote with her husband, and it became one of the most popular adventure stories of the early twentieth century. Orczy invented a type of hero that appeared over and over in later fiction: the ordinary-seeming person who is secretly brave, working in disguise to do what others will not.

The historical setting is real: the Reign of Terror (1793-1794) was a period of radical violence during the French Revolution in which tens of thousands of people, many of them nobles, were executed. Orczy sets her story just before the Terror’s worst months, in the autumn of 1792, when the pattern of fear and escape was already in motion.

💡 CONTEXT TO KEEP IN MIND
In Chapter 1, Orczy tells you the estate system has collapsed and the nobility is condemned as a class, not for individual crimes. The crowd cheers not because they know the people being executed, but because they have learned to see an entire social rank as the enemy.
📚 PART 3 · READ & NOTICE: NOW READ CHAPTER 1

Read Chapter 1 now. It opens at the West Barricade of Paris, where Sergeant Bibot guards the gate. You hear stories of aristos (nobles) trying to escape in disguise and the mysterious Englishman who organizes the rescues. Bibot tells the crowd how the previous sergeant, Grospierre, was tricked: a cart with hidden nobles got through because the Pimpernel himself posed as the captain who came to arrest them. The chapter ends with the news that Bibot, too, has just been fooled. Notice how Orczy builds the Pimpernel’s legend through stories the characters tell each other, not by showing him directly.

Commonly Confused Words III: affect/effect and than/then
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two pairs of words trip up even confident writers: affect/effect and than/then. Today you will learn a quick swap test for each pair and practice choosing the right word in sentences drawn from The Scarlet Pimpernel.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (inspired by Ch. 14)
"The decision she made at the ball would affect Armand's chances more than she knew then."
This sentence contains three of today's target words. You will come back to it after the lesson.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before the lesson: which pair trips you up more, affect/effect or than/then? (No wrong answer.)
💡 THE TWO RULES TO REMEMBER
Pair 1: affect / effect
affect (verb) = to influence
"The choice affected her."
Swap test: Can you replace it with "influence"? Yes? Use affect.
effect (noun) = the result
"The effect was silence."
Swap test: Can you replace it with "result"? Yes? Use effect.
Pair 2: than / then
than (comparison)
"She knew more than he did."
Use when two things are being compared.
then (time or sequence)
"She read it, then she froze."
Use when moving through time or steps.
📚 HOW BOTH PAIRS WORK

Affect and effect share almost all their letters, which is why readers mix them up. Affect is almost always a verb: it means to influence or have an impact on something. Effect is almost always a noun: it names the result or outcome. A quick swap test works for both: if you can replace the word with "influence" and the sentence still makes sense, use affect; if you can replace it with "result," use effect. The than/then pair trips writers up because the words sound nearly the same. Than signals a comparison between two things: Chauvelin is sharper than he appears. Then signals time or sequence: Marguerite read the note, then she hid it. If the sentence is comparing two things, than is correct. If the sentence is moving through time or steps, then is correct.

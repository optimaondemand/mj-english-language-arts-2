💡 THE ONE IDEA TO HOLD ONTO: STATIC VS DYNAMIC
Static character
Stays essentially the same from beginning to end. Their beliefs, values, and behavior do not shift in any important way. They may be major or minor characters.
Ask: Is this person the same kind of person they were at the start?
Dynamic character
Undergoes a meaningful internal change during the story. Something they experience shifts how they see themselves, others, or the world. The change is shown in their words or actions.
Ask: What caused the change, and how do we see it?
Important: neither label is better than the other. A static villain or a static loyal friend can be essential to a story. The label is a tool for analysis, not a judgment.
📚 PART 2 · READ & NOTICE: APPLYING THE TOOL TO THE NOVEL

What the characters do in the climax tells you who they are

In Chapters 29 and 30, Marguerite faces her worst moment: gagged and pinioned beside a boulder, she knows that screaming to warn Percy will get her brother Armand shot; staying silent means watching Percy walk into the trap. When she finally hears Percy’s voice singing "God Save the King," she cannot hold back. She screams, hammers at the hut walls, and shouts his name. This is a Marguerite the reader has been watching grow across the whole novel: from the woman who acted first and suffered the consequences (telling Chauvelin about the Pimpernel to save Armand) to someone who still acts on love, but now at enormous personal cost and with full knowledge of what she is risking. Her transformation is the argument of the whole second half of the book: she began as a woman who thought courage meant cleverness; she ends as a woman who discovers that it means sacrifice. Chauvelin, by contrast, is the same from his first scene to his last. He is remorseless, calculating, and ultimately outsmarted. His staticness is part of his function: he is the obstacle that reveals what Marguerite and Percy are made of.

📚 PART 3 · READ & NOTICE: NOW READ CHAPTERS 29–30

Read Chapters 29 and 30 now. In Chapter 29, Marguerite sits beside Chauvelin in the dark, trapped by his ultimatum. She hears Percy singing "God Save the King" as he approaches. She cannot stay silent. In Chapter 30, she screams, is thrown to the ground, and the hut is found empty: the fugitives have already gone. The Scarlet Pimpernel has outsmarted everyone again.

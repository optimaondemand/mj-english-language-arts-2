📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
A complex sentence joins one complete thought with a dependent clause -- a partial thought that cannot stand alone. Today you will learn where the comma goes when you start with the dependent clause, using a sentence from Chapter 23.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (from Chapter 23)
“As soon as he is aware of his danger, he will exercise more caution.”
Sir Andrew to Marguerite, about Percy
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
How confident are you with commas in sentences that start with words like “although,” “because,” or “as soon as”?
💡 ONE RULE FOR COMPLEX SENTENCES
One Rule for Complex Sentences
DEPENDENT CLAUSE
Starts with a subordinating conjunction (as soon as, although, because, when, if, since...). Cannot stand alone as a sentence.
INDEPENDENT CLAUSE
A complete sentence on its own. Has a subject, a verb, and a complete thought.
How the sentence breaks down:
As soon as he is aware of his danger,
dependent clause -- starts with “as soon as”
he will exercise more caution.
independent clause
👉 Comma here -- between the two clauses when the dependent clause comes first.
📚 HOW TO FIND WHERE THE COMMA GOES
  1. Find the verb. Ask: “What is being said about he (the subject)?” He will exercise. The verb phrase is will exercise. He is the subject pronoun. Will is the helping verb; exercise is the main verb.
  2. Find the subordinating conjunction. The words as soon as begin a dependent clause -- a partial thought that leans on the main sentence to be complete.
  3. Ask: which clause comes first? The dependent clause (“As soon as...danger”) comes first. When the dependent clause is first, place a comma after it, right before the independent clause begins.
  4. Reversed order -- no comma needed. “He will exercise more caution as soon as he is aware of his danger” -- when the independent clause comes first, no comma is needed.

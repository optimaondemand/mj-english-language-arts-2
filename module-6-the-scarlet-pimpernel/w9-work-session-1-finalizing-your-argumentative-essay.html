💡 THE SHAPE OF YOUR ESSAY
An argumentative essay makes a claim, backs it up with evidence, addresses the other side, and closes with a conclusion that says what it all means. Your Quick Writes built each part. Here is how they connect.
PART 1 · THESIS (from Quick Write 3, refined in your Week 8 outline)
State your claim about what kind of courage the novel celebrates. Name whether the Pimpernel’s disguise is heroic, deceptive, or both. One clear sentence that tells the reader exactly what you will argue.
PART 2 · EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION, FIRST PIECE (from Quick Write 4)
Give one specific detail from the novel. Then write a sentence that says what that detail shows about courage or disguise. The explanation is the part that does the work.
PART 3 · EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION, SECOND PIECE (from your Week 8 outline)
Add a second detail that adds to or deepens your first point. Explain how it extends your argument. Two pieces of evidence are stronger than one.
PART 4 · COUNTERARGUMENT (from Quick Write 5)
Acknowledge the strongest argument against your claim. Name what someone might say who disagrees. Then explain why your argument still holds. This is what makes a reader trust you.
PART 5 · CONCLUSION (from Quick Write 7)
Restate your point in fresh words. Say what the novel’s answer to the question means beyond the story. Do not simply repeat the thesis. End with something the reader can carry away.
📚 READ & NOTICE: A WORKED EXAMPLE

Here is a short argument that uses the same five-part shape. Study the labeled moves. You will not copy this thesis or these details. It is a model for how the parts connect, not an answer to borrow.

THESIS The novel argues that the most courageous acts are the ones that cost the hero something invisible: not blood, but reputation, since the Pimpernel must let people think him a fool while he risks his life for strangers.

EVIDENCE 1 Marguerite laughs at her husband Percy’s ridiculous posturing at the dinner table, never guessing he is the Pimpernel. (Explanation:) Orczy shows that the disguise requires Percy to sacrifice his wife’s respect, which turns ordinary bravery into a deeper kind of courage: one that cannot be seen or praised.

EVIDENCE 2 The Pimpernel’s plans succeed precisely because no one believes he is capable of them. (Explanation:) This means his heroism depends on others underestimating him, which is a kind of courage that looks like cowardice from the outside.

COUNTERARGUMENT Some readers might argue that disguise is a form of deception and that true heroism should be open and honest. That is a reasonable objection. But Orczy answers it through the novel’s outcome: the disguise saves lives precisely because honesty would cost them. The moral weight lands on the lives saved, not on the method used to save them.

CONCLUSION The novel asks us to consider a courage that cannot announce itself, and in doing so it argues that some of the most heroic acts in history are the ones that looked, at the time, like nothing at all.

Notice that every piece of evidence is followed by an explanation sentence that says what it means about courage. The counterargument does not simply name the opposing view. It gives it a fair hearing and then explains why the argument holds anyway. Those two moves are what separate a strong essay from a weak one.

📚 CHECK YOUR OWN THINKING

Read this sentence, which is meant to be a counterargument in a student essay:

“Some people might disagree with my claim.”

In your notebook, write down two things this sentence is missing, and then rewrite it so it actually does the work of a counterargument.

A counterargument needs to do three things: name the opposing view specifically, give it a fair hearing, and then explain why your claim still holds. “Some people might disagree” does none of these. It does not say what people would argue, it does not take that view seriously, and it gives no reason why the original claim survives the objection. Your rewrite should start with the actual objection someone could make about heroism, disguise, or courage in The Scarlet Pimpernel.

⚡ TRY IT · NAME EACH PART OF THE ARGUMENT

Below are five sentences from a short essay on courage in The Scarlet Pimpernel, out of order. For each one, decide which part of the shape it is: thesis, evidence, explanation, counterargument, or conclusion. Decide in your head first, then choose.

1. “It is fair to say that deception, even for good ends, raises hard moral questions. But in this novel, the deception saves lives that honesty would have ended, and Orczy keeps the moral weight on the outcome, not the method.”
2. “The Scarlet Pimpernel argues that the deepest courage is the kind that no one can see: it requires the hero to let others think the worst of him while he risks everything in secret.”
3. “This means that Percy’s disguise is not cowardice but a different kind of bravery: one that requires him to absorb contempt instead of admiration.”
4. “Orczy leaves us with a question the novel cannot fully answer: how much of what we call heroism depends on other people knowing about it? The Pimpernel is heroic precisely because he never lets them find out.”
5. “When Chauvelin watches Percy pretend to fuss over a floral pattern on his sleeve while men’s lives hang in the balance, Percy never breaks character.”
🧠 NOW BUILD YOUR ESSAY

Open your outline, your Quick Writes, and the Scarlet Pimpernel Final Project - Essay (assignment) page. Work through the five parts in order:

Thesis: take your refined thesis from your Week 8 outline (8.01). Make sure it names the kind of courage the novel celebrates and takes a clear position on whether the Pimpernel’s disguise is heroic or deceptive.
Evidence 1 and its explanation: pull your strongest detail from Quick Write 4. Write the sentence that says what it shows about courage. The explanation sentence is the most important sentence in that paragraph.
Evidence 2 and its explanation: add the second evidence point from your Week 8 outline (8.02) and explain how it extends your argument, not just repeats it.
Counterargument: use Quick Write 5. Name the opposing view specifically, give it a fair hearing, and then explain why your claim holds anyway.
Conclusion: use Quick Write 7. Restate the point in new words and say what it means about courage, heroism, or disguise beyond the novel’s plot.
💪 BEFORE YOU GO

Your outline and Quick Writes did the planning. All you are doing now is writing it out in full and making sure every explanation sentence reaches past the detail and names the idea. Thesis, two pieces of evidence with real explanation, a counterargument that takes the other side seriously, and a conclusion that says what it all means. Get that on the page and the essay is done.

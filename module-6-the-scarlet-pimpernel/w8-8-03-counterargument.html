📖 WHAT THIS LESSON IS FOR
A strong argument does not ignore the other side. It faces it and answers it.
Today you name the strongest objection to your thesis, state it fairly, and write a response that shows why your argument still holds. A counterargument is not a flaw in your essay. It is a sign that you are thinking like a careful reader. By the end you will add this section to your outline document.

💡 THE THREE MOVES
A counterargument section in your outline needs three moves in order.
MOVE 1 · STATE THE OTHER SIDE FAIRLY
Name the strongest objection to your thesis. If you argue the disguise is heroic, state the strongest reason someone might think it is deceptive or wrong. If you argue it is deceptive, state the strongest reason someone might think it is heroic. Use language like: "One could argue that..." or "A reader might point out that..." Do not set up a weak version of the other side just to knock it down easily.
MOVE 2 · GRANT WHAT IS TRUE
Acknowledge what is right about the objection. Language like: "This is a fair point" or "It is true that..." Readers trust an arguer more when they see that the arguer knows the other side has some merit. Skipping this move makes your response look like you did not really understand the objection.
MOVE 3 · EXPLAIN WHY YOUR ARGUMENT STILL HOLDS
Use language like: "However..." or "Even so..." Then explain why the objection does not overturn your thesis. You are not saying the other side is completely wrong. You are saying your argument is still the better reading of what Orczy shows.
📚 READ AND NOTICE: A WORKED EXAMPLE

Here is a complete counterargument section written for an essay arguing the disguise is heroic. Notice all three moves and how they connect. This is a model for the structure, not an answer to copy.

MOVE 1: OTHER SIDE One could argue that the Pimpernel's disguise is deceptive rather than heroic, because it requires lying not only to his enemies but also to Marguerite, the person he claims to love most.
MOVE 2: GRANT This is a fair point. The novel does show that Marguerite suffers genuine hurt from Percy's apparent coldness, and that pain is real, not a minor cost.
MOVE 3: RESPONSE However, Orczy makes clear that Percy keeps the secret specifically to protect Marguerite from Chauvelin's threats, not out of disrespect or indifference. The deception is not the point of the disguise; it is the price of it. The novel asks readers to see that some forms of love require a sacrifice the other person cannot even know about, and that sacrifice is what makes the disguise heroic rather than merely dishonest.

Notice that Move 3 does not just repeat the thesis. It answers the specific objection and explains why the evidence still supports the original argument. Your response should do the same.

⚡ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

For each sentence, identify which of the three counterargument moves it is performing.

1. “However, Orczy consistently frames the League’s secrecy as the condition of its effectiveness: exposure means deaths, not just arrests.”
2. “A reader might argue that the Pimpernel’s secrecy is a form of arrogance: he decides alone that Marguerite cannot be trusted with the truth.”
3. “It is true that Percy’s silence causes Marguerite real suffering, and the novel does not minimize that cost.”
🔍 BEFORE YOU BUILD: CHECK YOUR OWN REASONING
Back in Module 2, you learned to spot three ways an argument can go wrong: attacking the person instead of the argument, offering a false either/or when more real options exist, and making a hasty generalization from just one example. Those same problems can creep into your own counterargument section. Look back at the Move 1 objection you are about to write. Is it the strongest real version of the opposing view, or has it quietly turned into a weak, easy-to-beat version (a straw man) or an attack on a person instead of an idea? Fix Move 1 now, before you write Moves 2 and 3, if it needs to be fairer.
🧠 NOW ADD YOUR COUNTERARGUMENT TO THE OUTLINE

Open your Quick Write 5 and your outline document. Below your evidence points, add a counterargument section with all three moves. Label each move in the outline so you can find them easily when you write the essay next week.

Pick the strongest objection, not the easiest one. If your response can handle the strongest version, your argument is solid.
Save the updated outline to OneDrive when you are done.

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Chauvelin seizes two members of the League, reads a letter that proves Armand is a traitor to France, and tells Marguerite: find the Scarlet Pimpernel, or your brother dies.
Chapters 9 and 10 shift between two locations and two sets of characters, and then they converge. In Chapter 9, Chauvelin's spies make their move at the Fisherman's Rest. In Chapter 10, Marguerite sits in her opera box at Covent Garden and learns what the night has cost. Today you will trace how Orczy builds this trap and how Marguerite is caught inside it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain how Chauvelin uses the scene at the Fisherman's Rest to gain leverage over Marguerite.
2
I will be able to describe Marguerite's dilemma at the opera and the choice Chauvelin forces on her.
3
I will be able to evaluate what Marguerite's decision to comply tells us about loyalty and what it costs.

💡 HOW CHAUVELIN BUILDS THE TRAP
1️⃣
Ch. 8: The Request
Chauvelin asks Marguerite politely. She refuses.
2️⃣
Ch. 9: The Seizure
Spies ambush Lord Tony and Sir Andrew, seize their papers. Armand's letter is in the pocketbook.
3️⃣
Ch. 10: The Ultimatum
Chauvelin tells Marguerite: Armand's letter proves treason. Help me tonight, or he dies.
The trap works because Chauvelin acted between Chapters 8 and 10. By the time Marguerite hears the news, it is already done.
📚 PART 1 · READ & NOTICE: CHAPTER 9 AT THE INN
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
At the start of Chapter 9, the two young men, Lord Tony and Sir Andrew, are relaxed in the firelit coffee-room, reading their instructions and talking quietly. Why does that feel tense even before the attack comes?

The figure on the floor

Read Chapter 9 now. Notice how Orczy handles the attack itself: she spends more time on the calm before it than on the violence. By the time Lord Tony and Sir Andrew are bound and gagged, we have seen the masked figure slither across the floor in the dark for an entire paragraph. When it is over, Chauvelin quietly removes his mask, reads the seized papers by the dying firelight, and finds Armand's letter. He mutters to himself: "Armand St. Just a traitor after all." Then: "Now, fair Marguerite Blakeney, I think that you will help me to find the Scarlet Pimpernel." Everything that follows in Chapter 10 flows from those two sentences.

Add to the Week 3 section of your Reading Journal: How does Chauvelin use the events of Chapter 9 to force Marguerite's hand in Chapter 10? Write 2 to 3 sentences explaining the connection between the seized papers and the ultimatum at the opera.
📚 PART 2 · READ & NOTICE: CHAPTER 10 AT THE OPERA

"You must help me tonight"

Chapter 10 opens at Covent Garden Theatre, London, during a performance of Gluck's Orpheus. Orczy plants the irony carefully: Orpheus is a story about a man who descends into the underworld to rescue the person he loves. Marguerite is surrounded by music about heroic love and impossible rescue, just as Chauvelin slips into her box to tell her that Armand is doomed unless she acts. She keeps her face pleasant and her voice steady while absorbing news that could shatter her. That control, Orczy suggests, is its own form of courage.

The ultimatum is precise: Chauvelin has a small note, found with the seized papers, signed with a tiny red flower. The Scarlet Pimpernel will be at Lord Grenville's ball tonight. Watch for him. Find out who he is. If she succeeds, Armand goes free. If she refuses, the letter implicating Armand goes to the Committee of Public Safety. Marguerite knows Chauvelin is not bluffing. She agrees.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND MORAL DILEMMAS
An AI tool can quickly summarize the plot of Chapter 10 or list the reasons Marguerite agrees to Chauvelin's terms. What it cannot do is sit with the discomfort of her choice. Deciding whether Marguerite is right to comply, what she should have done differently, and whether loyalty to family can ever override other obligations: that thinking is yours. It is also exactly what your Quick Write 3 will ask you to start on Day 5.
⚡ TRY IT · WHAT KIND OF COURAGE?
At the end of Chapter 10, Marguerite agrees to spy for Chauvelin to save Armand. Several ways to describe that decision are listed below. For each one, decide: does Orczy's text support this reading, or does the text point against it?
1. Marguerite agrees because she has no real choice. She is not a traitor; she is trapped.
2. Marguerite agrees because she is ambitious and sees a chance to gain influence.
3. Marguerite's ability to stay calm while hearing the worst news of her life is itself a form of courage.

Lesson 3.03 · Weekly Workshop
What Do Heroes Risk? Building a Thesis
Lesson ≈12 min
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every character in Chapters 7–10 is risking something: Armand risks his life for a cause, Marguerite risks betraying a hero to save her brother, and the Scarlet Pimpernel risks everything in disguise. What does the novel say about what that kind of risk looks like from the inside?
This short lesson prepares you to write Quick Write 3, your first thesis for the module project. A thesis is a one-sentence claim that makes an argument: not just a topic or a summary, but a position you can defend with evidence from the text.
💡 WHAT A THESIS LOOKS LIKE
A TOPIC (not a thesis):
"The novel is about loyalty."
This is true, but it does not make a claim. Anyone could agree without thinking.
➞
A THESIS (arguable claim + reason):
"In The Scarlet Pimpernel, true heroism requires not just courage but the willingness to act invisibly, without recognition, for the sake of others."
This takes a position. Someone could disagree. Now you need evidence to support it.
The test: Could a reasonable person read the same novel and disagree with your claim? If yes, you have a thesis. If anyone would instantly agree without looking at the text, you have a topic, not a thesis.
📚 PART 1 · READ & NOTICE: WHAT THE NOVEL SAYS ABOUT HEROES
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
After reading Chapters 7 through 10, which of these feels closest to what Orczy is arguing about heroism in this novel? (This is a thinking warm-up, not a quiz. Pick the one that resonates most for you right now.)

Looking across the week's reading

Chapters 7 through 10 have given you three very different kinds of risk. Armand is going back to France even though it is dangerous, out of obligation to his country. Marguerite agrees to spy on the Pimpernel even though she admires him, out of obligation to her brother. The Pimpernel, whoever he is, disguises himself completely and disappears into anonymity out of obligation to strangers. Orczy is not saying all three are equally heroic. She is building a novel-long argument about which kind of courage she believes is highest. Your Quick Write 3 asks you to take a position on that argument using evidence from the text.

💪 BEFORE YOU GO

The thesis you draft today is the seed of your final argumentative essay. You will come back to it in later weeks, test it against new evidence, refine it, and eventually build it into a full argument. Write the most honest position you can defend right now, and do not worry about getting it perfect. A good thesis that can be improved is worth more than a safe summary that can never be challenged.

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Percy was the Jew driving the cart. He watched Chauvelin the whole time. The fugitives were already on the boat when Marguerite screamed. What does all of this tell us about the kind of hero Orczy is celebrating?
Today you will finish Chapter 31, pull together your reading of the whole novel, and write the conclusion paragraph for your argument.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED
▶
The Scarlet Pimpernel, Chapter 31. [Teacher: link the Chapter 31 file here] · Your Reading Journal · Your Microsoft Word document for Quick Write 7.
📚 PART 1 · READ & NOTICE: BEFORE YOU BEGIN
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read the last chapter: at this point in the novel, who do you think has shown more courage, Percy or Marguerite? (No wrong answer. Just commit to a position before you see how it ends.)
💡 WHAT CHAPTER 31 REVEALS: THE PLAN INSIDE THE PLAN
What Chauvelin believed
Percy was somewhere on the roads or cliffs. He would eventually walk into the trap around the hut.
What Percy actually did
Percy WAS the Jew. He drove Chauvelin. He slipped the note into the hut. The fugitives left before the soldiers arrived. He waited for Marguerite.
The fake note
Percy left a second note directing Chauvelin to the wrong creek. While Chauvelin’s men ran there, Percy and Marguerite and Sir Andrew walked to the real meeting point.
The intelligence behind the escape is not just physical bravery: it is Percy understanding how obedient soldiers follow orders, how Chauvelin reads evidence, and how to use all of that against him.
📚 PART 2 · READ & NOTICE: NOW READ CHAPTER 31

Read Chapter 31 now. Marguerite hears a voice, British and familiar, cursing in the dark. She realizes with disbelief that the half-beaten, rope-trussed figure lying against the rocks is Percy in his Jew’s disguise. She frees him. He explains the whole plan: he drove Chauvelin, slipped the note through the hut wall, watched the soldiers wait in obedience while the fugitives escaped, and then signaled with the song while he stayed behind to find Marguerite. When he had drunk some brandy, he admitted: he knew everything all along, including her role in the betrayal. He has already forgiven her. Sir Andrew Ffoulkes arrives, and the three of them make their way along the cliffs to a second boat waiting at a different creek. By dawn they are on the Daydream. The novel ends with a brief summary: Armand is safe, Chauvelin returns to France in disgrace, and Sir Andrew marries Suzanne de Tournay.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE ENDING ARGUE?
Chapter 31 reveals Percy’s full plan. For each claim below, decide whether the ending of the novel supports it or complicates it. Decide first, then click.
1. The novel argues that the Pimpernel’s courage is primarily in his intelligence and planning, not just his physical bravery.
2. The novel argues that the Pimpernel’s disguise was entirely heroic and had no moral cost.
💡 WORTH KNOWING · USING AI TOOLS FOR ARGUMENT WORK
Some students use AI writing tools to help generate argument ideas or conclusion paragraphs. What those tools cannot do is read the specific moments in Chapters 27–31 that you have actually read, notice what surprised you, or connect the evidence to your own claim. Your Quick Write 7 conclusion will be stronger if it draws on your own notes from this week, not a generated draft. Use these tools for understanding a concept or checking a sentence structure, not for replacing the interpretive work.

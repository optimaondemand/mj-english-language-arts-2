Subject-Verb Agreement: One Rule, Every Sentence
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 FOCUS
A verb must agree with its subject noun. The challenge is that a prepositional phrase often lands between them, and the nearest noun is not always the subject. One tool works on every sentence.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (inspired by Chapter 11)
“The members of the League of the Scarlet Pimpernel carry their secret without a word.”
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Which sentence is more likely to be written?
💡 THE AGREEMENT RULE
THE AGREEMENT RULE
THE RULE
A verb must agree with its subject noun, not with the nearest noun.
THE TEST
Find the subject. Ask: “Who or what [verb]?” The answer is the subject noun. Then check: singular subject = singular verb; plural subject = plural verb.
WATCH OUT
Watch out for prepositional phrases between the subject and verb. The noun inside the phrase is the object of the preposition. It is not the subject.
EXAMPLES FROM THE WEEK'S SENTENCE:
The members [of the League] carry their secret.
Subject noun: members (plural) — verb: carry (plural). Correct.
The leader [of the agents] speaks for all.
Subject noun: leader (singular) — verb: speaks (singular). Correct.
📚 HOW TO FIND THE SUBJECT AND MATCH THE VERB

Watch the questioning flow on this week’s sentence.

  1. What is being said about the members? They carry. The word carry is the verb, specifically an action verb.
  2. Who or what carry? The members. Members is the subject noun.
  3. Are members singular or plural? Plural. So the verb must be plural: carry. Correct.
  4. What about the phrase in between? The phrase “of the League of the Scarlet Pimpernel” comes between members and carry. The nouns League and Pimpernel inside that phrase are objects of the preposition of. They are not the subject. The verb agrees with members, not with League or Pimpernel.

⚡ TRY IT · SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT
For each sentence, choose the correct verb. Ask “Who or what [verb]?” to find the subject noun first, then check singular or plural.
1. The courage of these men ___ beyond anything Chauvelin expected.
2. Sir Andrew Ffoulkes and Lord Antony Dewhurst ___ at the ball, watching for their chief.
3. Neither the lights nor the noise ___ Chauvelin from his purpose.
4. Everyone in the grand rooms ___ the doggerel verse by heart.

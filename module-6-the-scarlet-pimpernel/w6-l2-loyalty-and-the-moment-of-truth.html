📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In Chapter 23, Sir Andrew reminds Marguerite of something she had forgotten: Percy made a promise to the fugitives, and he will keep it no matter what. This lesson looks closely at what that kind of loyalty costs, and at one sentence from Chapter 25 that shows us who Percy really is.
⚡ READ & NOTICE · QUICK CHECK-IN
QUICK CHECK-IN
Before we look at the passage closely: in your view, is Percy right to go back for the fugitives even knowing Chauvelin is after him?
💡 THE MOMENT OF LOYALTY
What Marguerite wanted to do
When she heard Percy might be captured, she thought only of him. She forgot Armand and the Comte entirely. "With the sublime selfishness of a woman who loves with her whole heart, she had in the last twenty-four hours had no thought save for him." (Ch 23)
What Sir Andrew said Percy would do
Percy pledged his honour. He will not break it. Not for safety. Not for Marguerite. The whole League trusts him because he never has.
📖 READ & NOTICE · CLOSE READING: ONE KEY PASSAGE
"...Sir Percy Blakeney, Bart., might have been on his way to a garden-party at the Prince of Wales', instead of deliberately, cold-bloodedly running his head in a trap, set for him by his deadliest enemy."
(Ch 25)

This sentence is doing two things at once. On the surface it describes Percy's clothes and manner: impeccable, aristocratic, entirely at ease. But the phrase "cold-bloodedly" breaks the surface. It tells us this apparent ease is a performance, a deliberate one, and that the real Percy underneath is calculating every move. Orczy puts the word "deliberately" right next to "cold-bloodedly" to show that there is nothing reckless about Percy's behavior. He is not walking into a trap because he is foolish. He is walking into it because he has a plan and because his promise to the fugitives demands it. The sentence asks you to hold two images of the same man at once: the laughing fop at a garden-party, and the man who chose this knowing exactly what it might cost.

📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Proofreading is reading your own writing carefully to catch errors. Editing is deciding how to fix them. Today you will practice both on sentences that contain errors drawn from this week’s grammar topics and from the novel, and you will apply that skill to your own Quick Write draft.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (from Chapter 31)
“It was the sound of a good, solid, absolutely British 'Damn!'”
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you reread your own writing, what kind of mistake do you most often catch? (No wrong answer.)
💡 A PROOFREADING CHECKLIST
1
Subject and verb agree. Find the subject noun or subject pronoun. Ask: "What is being said about [the subject]?" The verb must match (singular subject = singular verb; plural subject = plural verb).
2
Every modifier points to the right noun. If a phrase starts the sentence, ask who or what is doing that action: does the subject of the sentence match?
3
Pronouns match their nouns. Ask: who does this pronoun replace? Check that the pronoun’s number (singular/plural) and person (he/she/they) match the noun it replaces.
4
Punctuation ends every sentence and joins correctly. Check for missing end marks and for comma splices (two full sentences joined by only a comma).
📚 APPLYING THE CHECKLIST TO THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE

Here is the sentence from Chapter 31, correctly classified:

Classified sentence:
It
subject pronoun was
linking verb the
article adjective sound
subject noun of a good, solid, absolutely British
adjective phrase “Damn!”
noun (quoted word)

Notice that the subject pronoun It refers to the sound Marguerite has just heard. The linking verb was connects the pronoun to its description. All four checklist items are satisfied: subject and verb agree (singular "it" / singular "was"); no modifier problem; pronoun matches its referent; punctuation is correct. This is what a clean, edited sentence looks like.

Commonly Confused Words II: its / it's and to / too / two
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 FOCUS
The same rule from the previous grammar lesson applies again: possessive pronouns have no apostrophe. A second set is added: the three homophones to, too, and two.

💡 THE ONE RULE TO REMEMBER (AGAIN)
Possessive pronouns never have apostrophes.
Contractions always do. Numbers and "also" are neither.
its / it's
its = belongs to it (possessive pronoun)
The League had its own secret signal.

it's = it is (contraction)
It's a dangerous business, rescuing aristocrats.

Quick test: replace with "it is." If it still makes sense, use it's. If not, use its.
to / too / two
two = the number 2. Always.
Chauvelin had two spies in the room.

too = also, or excessively
Lord Tony was taken, and Sir Andrew too.

to = direction, purpose, infinitive (everything else)
She walked to the edge of the cliff.
Memory hooks: Two has a "w," just like the word "twin." Too has an extra "o," suggesting something extra or excessive. To is the short, plain one that covers everything else.
📚 AN ORIGINAL SENTENCE, CLASSIFIED

Here is an original example sentence combining words from both today's sets. Watch how each word is classified.

"It's too dangerous to send two messengers; the League must protect its members."
Classified sentence:
It's
contraction = it is too
adverb (excessively) dangerous
adjective to
infinitive marker send
verb two
adjective (number) messengers;
noun the
article adjective League
subject noun must protect
verb phrase its
possessive pronoun members.
noun

Notice: It's (contraction, = "it is") vs. its (possessive, no apostrophe). Too (excessively) and two (the number) vs. to (infinitive marker).

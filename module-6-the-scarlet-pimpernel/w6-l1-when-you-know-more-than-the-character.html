When You Know More Than the Character

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Chapters 23 and 24 put you in an unusual position: you know exactly what Chauvelin is planning, you can see the trap being set, and you watch Percy walk straight into it, cheerfully, singing "God Save the King."
You will name the kind of irony that creates this feeling and start noticing how Orczy uses it.
📚 READ & NOTICE: BEFORE YOU BEGIN
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you were reading Chapter 24 and Percy walked in singing, what did you feel?
💡 THE ONE IDEA TO HOLD ONTO
Who knows… What they know
The Reader (you) Chauvelin’s full trap: all roads patrolled, Desgas returning with soldiers, the plan to catch Percy at Père Blanchard’s hut
Marguerite (in the loft) Chauvelin’s plan: she overheard it all. But she is hidden and cannot speak without ruining Percy’s chances.
Percy (entering the inn) He sees Chauvelin. He does not let on what he knows, if anything.
The gap between what we know and what the characters know is called dramatic irony.
📚 READ & NOTICE: HOW DRAMATIC IRONY WORKS IN THESE CHAPTERS

In Chapter 23, Sir Andrew warns Marguerite that Chauvelin followed them from Dover disguised as a curé. In Chapter 24, Chauvelin arrives at the Chat Gris, orders the room cleared, and lays out his plan to Desgas in careful detail: the roads are patrolled, the soldiers will shadow Percy to the hut, and there they will spring the trap. Marguerite listens from the loft above, unable to make a sound. When Percy then walks in, dressed impeccably, laughing, singing, the contrast between what we know and what appears to be happening creates an almost unbearable suspense. Orczy is in full control of information: she decides what the reader knows and when, so that we feel the danger more sharply than any character does.

✏️ READING JOURNAL · WEEK 6
Open the Week 6 section of your Reading Journal. Write your answers to these two questions after you have read Chapters 23 and 24.

2. How does Orczy’s choice to give the reader Chauvelin’s full plan (in his conversation with Desgas) affect the way you feel watching Percy enter?

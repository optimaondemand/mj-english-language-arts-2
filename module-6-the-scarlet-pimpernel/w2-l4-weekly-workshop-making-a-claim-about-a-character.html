Lesson 2.04 · Weekly Workshop
Making a Claim About a Character
Lesson ≈10 min
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
An argument begins with a claim: one sentence that says something specific and debatable about the text. This week you have been gathering evidence about Sir Percy and Marguerite. Today you will shape that evidence into a claim. Your Quick Write 2 will grow from it.
💡 WHAT MAKES A STRONG CLAIM
A strong claim about a character does three things. It names the character. It says something specific that is not just a plot summary. And it is debatable: a careful reader could disagree.
Too weak (fact, not claim)
“Sir Percy Blakeney is a character in the novel.”
Too vague (not debatable)
“Sir Percy is an interesting person.”
Strong claim (specific + debatable)
“Sir Percy Blakeney’s performance as a fool is itself a form of courage, requiring more self-control than any open act of bravery.”

Notice what the strong claim does: it takes a position on what his behavior means, not just what it is. A reader could disagree, which makes it worth arguing.

⚡ TRY IT · STRONG CLAIM OR WEAK CLAIM?

Each statement below is about a character from Chapters 3 through 6. Decide whether it is a strong claim or a weak one. Think it over before you click.

1. “Marguerite’s composure after the Comtesse’s insult reveals a kind of courage that is different from the League’s physical risk.”
2. “The Comtesse de Tournay refuses to let Suzanne speak to Marguerite.”
3. “When Lord Antony calls the League’s work ‘sport,’ he is deflecting attention rather than telling the truth about his motives.”

Consistent Verb Tense: Keeping Your Verbs in Step
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
A story moves through time. The tense you choose tells the reader when things happen. Today you will learn to keep all verbs in the same tense so your writing stays clear. You will be able to spot and fix tense shifts.
📚 SENTENCE FROM THE BOOK (Chapter 19)
“She tore open the envelope and read.”
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before this lesson: do you ever catch yourself switching from past to present tense in the middle of a sentence when writing about a story? (No wrong answer.)
💡 THE TENSE RULE
CONSISTENT
“She tore open the envelope and read.”
Both verbs use past tense. The reader stays in one moment in time.
SHIFTED: WATCH FOR THIS
“She tore open the envelope and reads.”
Tore is past. Reads is present. The reader is suddenly confused about when this is happening.
The Rule: Pick one tense and stay in it. When writing about a novel, use past tense to match the author's narration.
📚 HOW TO CHECK VERB TENSE

In the sentence “She tore open the envelope and read,” there are two verbs: tore and read. Here is how to check them.

  1. Find the verbs.  “What is being said about she?” She tore and she read. Those are your two action verbs.
  2. Name the tense of each one.  Tore is past tense (the present form is tear). Read is also past tense (the past form of read is pronounced differently but spelled the same). Both past tense: consistent.
  3. What a tense shift looks like.  If the sentence read “She tore open the envelope and reads,” tore is past and reads is present. That shift confuses the reader about when the action is happening.
  4. The fix.  Pick one tense for the whole sentence and paragraph. For retelling story events, past tense (tore, read) is the natural choice because it matches how the novel is written.

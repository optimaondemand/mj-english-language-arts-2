You have read every chapter. Now you get to say what it all means. Today's term is thesis. A thesis is one sentence. First, it names a theme: loyalty, family, or staying gold. Second, it says what the novel shows about that theme. Third, a reader could disagree with it, so it needs proof. And fourth, the whole essay proves it, paragraph by paragraph. Here is my question. Which of the three themes do you have the most to say about?

Back at the chalkboard. An essay has parts, and each part has a job. The thesis says what the novel shows. The evidence points to one moment in the book. The explanation says what that moment shows, and that is where the credit is. The conclusion says the point again, in fresh words. Name each part, then fix one weak explanation.

2Which part of an essay is this sentence?Near the end of the novel, one of the main characters leaves behind a written message for Ponyboy about what staying gold means.
3Which part of an essay is this sentence?This reveals that staying gold is not about being young forever but about holding onto something good even when life tries to take it from you.
4Which part of an essay is this sentence?Throughout The Outsiders, family, whether blood or found, is the one thing that gives the characters a reason to keep going.

Now put the case in order. The introduction opens with a hook, gives a little context, and ends with your thesis. Each body takes one evidence sheet. The third body is up to you: a third moment, or the other side and why your thesis still holds. The conclusion closes in fresh words.

📌 Watch how: Priya’s outline
Priya
7th Grade Scholar
Introduction
Hook: Most people decide who you are before you say a word. Context: In S. E. Hinton’s The Outsiders, Ponyboy Curtis is a greaser, and the Socs are the rich kids from the West Side. Thesis: In The Outsiders, Hinton shows that the line between greasers and Socs is drawn by labels more than by who people are.
Body I
Point: A Soc can want the same things a greaser wants. Evidence: Randy says he is sick of the fighting and won’t show at the rumble (Hinton, Ch. 7).
Body II
Point: The label gets the blame even when the greasers did nothing. Evidence: the cafeteria (Hinton, Ch. 12).
A complication
Other side: Cherry still won’t say hi at school (Hinton, Ch. 3). Why it still holds: that shows how strong the label is, not who she is.
Conclusion
Fresh words: Greaser and Soc are names other people hand you. Whole novel: Ponyboy writes his whole story so people will stop judging by the name.

Commas and periods go inside
Inside the mark: yes“Hold on,”“near the back.”
Outside the mark: no“Hold on”,“near the back”.
Welcome to the chalkboard. Dialogue comes in three patterns. Speaker at the end: the words, a comma inside the closing mark, then who said it. Speaker at the start: who said it, a comma, then the words, with the end mark inside. Speaker in the middle: the tag splits one sentence, so the second part starts with a small letter. There is a seat, the driver said, near the back. And remember: commas and periods always go inside the marks.

📖 Priya’s draft, a model
Priya
7th Grade Scholar · the quiet climax

Everybody waits for the rumble, but the biggest moment in Chapter 9 is quiet. The greasers win the fight, and Darry says they beat the Socs. For a minute that seems like the climax. Then Dally drives Ponyboy to the hospital to see Johnny. When Dally says they won, Johnny does not even smile. He says fighting is no good (Hinton, Ch. 9). That is the real turn, because after it the win does not matter to anyone. Dally can’t take what happens next, and Ponyboy walks home in a daze in Chapter 10. Nobody in Chapter 10 celebrates the win. Some readers would say “the rumble is the climax”. I understand why, because the whole book builds up to that night and both gangs get ready for it. But a climax is where things can’t go back, and the rumble did not change the greasers. They won and they still lost. Johnny’s words changed Dally and Ponyboy for good. That is why I think the quiet moment is the true climax.

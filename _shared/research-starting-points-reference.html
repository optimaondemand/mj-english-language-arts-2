📖 WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
Whenever a lesson asks you to go find something out, this is the page to start from. It lists real, free, reputable places to look, not just the first thing a search engine hands you. Bookmark it now; you will use it again this year.
📚 JSTOR: A LIBRARY, NOT A SEARCH ENGINE

JStor is a large digital library of articles that other experts have checked before publishing. Getting the most out of it takes two steps.

STEP 1 · JSTOR DAILY (daily.jstor.org)
Free, no login. Short articles written for anyone, on real subjects, and every one links back to the scholarship behind it. Start here to find your angle.
STEP 2 · JSTOR (jstor.org)
Follow the link into the full article. You arrive with context instead of dropping in cold.

If you are researching something from the world of The Call of the Wild, start with two or three words, not a full question:

⚖️ JUDGE WHAT YOU FIND

A source being on JStor is a good sign, but you still check it. Use the same four questions from the How Arguments Work reference page, adjusted for a source instead of a character's argument:

  1. Who wrote it? A scholar, a journalist, a company trying to sell something? Look past the About page for outside confirmation.
  2. What does it claim? State the main point in one sentence, in your own words.
  3. What does it rest on? Primary documents, other named experts, or just an assertion with nothing behind it?
  4. Who was it written for? A general reader, other scholars, a customer? That shapes what it leaves in and out.
  5. What does it leave out? Name one thing a fuller account would need to mention.
✎ CHECK YOURSELF · WHICH SOURCE HOLDS UP BETTER?
1. You are researching gold rush food. Source A is a museum's history page with a named curator and a list of the ration records it used. Source B is a blog selling "authentic gold rush" camping gear, with no author listed. Which is the stronger source?
2. Source C is a university library guide to the Klondike Gold Rush with citations to primary documents. Source D is a forum post where someone says "I read somewhere that..." with no source named. Which is the stronger source?
✅ WORTH KNOWING · STUDY AIDS ARE NOT SOURCES
Sites that summarize a book or a topic for you (study-guide sites, for example) are useful for orientation but are not something to cite. They are a summary of someone else's reading, not the reading itself.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND SOURCES
An AI tool can help you turn a question into search words. It cannot tell you what sources actually exist, and it sometimes invents titles and authors that sound real but are not. Only use a source you found and opened yourself.

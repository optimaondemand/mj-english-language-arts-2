Correcting Dialogue: Catching the Mistakes Writers Make
Lesson · approx. 12 min
📖 FOCUS · READ & NOTICE
Knowing the three dialogue patterns makes it possible to catch a missed comma, a lost quotation mark, or a period in the wrong place. Correcting dialogue takes the same knowledge as writing it.

📖 PART 2 · THE THREE COMMON ERRORS

See the Difference: Before and After

When dialogue has an error, one small piece is out of place. The sentence below has a mistake. Compare the two versions and find what changed.

✗ BEFORE (ERROR)
"I left the door open." she said quietly.
Problem: The period after open should be a comma. When the dialogue tag follows the spoken words, you need a comma inside the quotation marks, not a period.
✓ AFTER (CORRECTED)
"I left the door open," she said quietly.
Fixed: Comma inside the closing quotation mark. The dialogue tag she said quietly becomes part of the same sentence.
ERROR TYPE 1: Missing Quotation Marks
Wrong: Marcus said, The train already left.
The spoken words need quotation marks around them. Without them, the reader cannot tell where Marcus's voice begins and ends.
Corrected: Marcus said, "The train already left."
ERROR TYPE 2: Period Before a Dialogue Tag (Should Be a Comma)
Wrong: "I'm ready." she said.
A period ends a sentence completely. But she said is a dialogue tag that is still part of the same sentence. Use a comma instead of a period before the closing quotation mark.
Corrected: "I'm ready," she said.

ERROR TYPE 3: Period Outside the Closing Quotation Mark
Wrong: She answered, "No one is waiting for us".
In American English, the period always goes inside the closing quotation mark, not outside it.
Corrected: She answered, "No one is waiting for us."

Dialogue Punctuation
Grammar · ~12 min
🎯 Today's Focus
"When a character speaks, you need punctuation to show where the words begin and end, and how to connect those words to the speaker."
Written dialogue follows a clear set of patterns. Once you recognize the three patterns, you can punctuate any line of dialogue correctly.
🎯 Objectives
  1. Identify where quotation marks, commas, and end punctuation go in a line of dialogue.
  2. Apply the three main dialogue patterns correctly in your own writing.
DIALOGUE PUNCTUATION: THREE PATTERNS
SPEAKER AT THE END
“I left my notebook on the bus,” Elena said.
quotation mark  ·  dialogue words  ·  comma inside closing quote  ·  dialogue tag  ·  period
SPEAKER AT THE START
Marco said, “We should leave before the rain starts.”
dialogue tag  ·  comma  ·  quotation mark  ·  dialogue words  ·  period inside closing quote
SPEAKER IN THE MIDDLE (INTERRUPTED)
“I thought,” Priya said, “that the meeting was tomorrow.”
first half of dialogue  ·  comma inside quote  ·  dialogue tag  ·  comma  ·  second half of dialogue  ·  period inside closing quote

📖 PART 2 · THE THREE PATTERNS

Three Patterns, Every Dialogue Sentence

Every line of written dialogue fits one of three patterns based on where the speaker's name (the dialogue tag) appears. The punctuation follows from the pattern. Learn the pattern, and the punctuation follows automatically.

Pattern 1: Speaker at the End

“The game starts at seven,” Dev said.

open quote
The game starts at seven,
dialogue words
”
comma + close quote
Dev

said
action verb

period
When the dialogue tag comes at the end, the comma goes inside the closing quotation mark, and the sentence ends with a period after the tag.

Pattern 2: Speaker at the Start

Elena said, “We will need more time than that.”
Elena
subject noun
said,
action verb + comma
“
open quote
We will need more time than that.
dialogue words + period
”
close quote
When the dialogue tag comes first, a comma follows the tag (outside the quotation mark), and the period goes inside the closing quotation mark.

Pattern 3: Speaker in the Middle (Interrupted)

“I think,” Marco said, “we are going the wrong direction.”
“
open quote
I think,
dialogue + comma
”
close quote
Marco
subject noun
said,
action verb + comma
“
open quote
we are going the wrong direction.
rest of dialogue + period
”
close quote
When the tag interrupts the dialogue, both halves get quotation marks. A comma closes the first half (inside the quote), and another comma follows the tag. The second half ends with a period inside its closing quotation mark.

Each sentence below has a dialogue punctuation question. Choose the correct answer. You will see feedback right away.
A student writes: “I finished the report ___ ” Priya said. Where does the comma go?
“I finished the report ___ ” Priya said.

📝 PART 4 · APPLY

Now try the patterns in your own writing.

In your Reading Journal, write two original sentences of dialogue: one with the speaker at the end (Pattern 1), and one with the speaker at the beginning (Pattern 2). Use characters you make up. Do not use characters from the novel.

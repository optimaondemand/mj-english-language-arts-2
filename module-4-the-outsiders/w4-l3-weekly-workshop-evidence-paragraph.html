Lesson 4.03 · Weekly Workshop
Writing an Evidence Paragraph
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
You will put this week’s evidence work together into one focused paragraph: a claim about theme, a quoted passage from your copy of the novel, and an explanation of what the passage shows. This paragraph is Quick Write 4 and will become part of your module project.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you look back at your VR stations and Reading Journal notes from this week, what feels clearest? What still feels unsettled?
📚 YOUR WRITING TASK TODAY

You have already done the hard work. You found a theme in Lessons 1 and 2, built a symbolic scene in VR Activity 1, and organized your evidence in VR Activity 2. Now you write it up as a tight evidence paragraph using the three-part move: claim, quote, explain.

Your paragraph should be 100–150 words. That is not a lot of space, so keep every sentence working. Start with your claim. Then introduce your quoted passage with a signal phrase. Then explain, in your own words, what the passage shows about your theme.

Check: Does your paragraph say how the passage supports your claim, not just that it does? That explanation is the most important sentence you will write.

➡️ WHAT YOU'LL DO NEXT
1
Quick Write 4 (Word document on OneDrive)
Open the Quick Write 4 assignment and write your evidence paragraph in Microsoft Word. Then complete the Check-up.
2
Check-up
Complete the Check-up quiz in Canvas to confirm this week’s reading and grammar have landed. Then check your submission list at the end of the quiz.
💪 KEEP GOING

The paragraph you write today will not just be practice. You will use it, or a stronger version of it, in your Module 4 project. Write it as if it already matters, because it does.

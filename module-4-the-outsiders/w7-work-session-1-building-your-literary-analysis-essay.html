📖 WHAT THIS SESSION IS FOR
This session shapes Quick Writes 3, 4, 5, and 6 into one literary-analysis essay plan on a theme from The Outsiders (loyalty, family, or staying gold). All evidence comes from the student’s copy of the novel. The plan can be typed into the The Outsiders Final Project - Essay (assignment) page. This session is practice and is not graded; the project is.

💡 THE SHAPE OF A LITERARY ANALYSIS ESSAY
A literary analysis essay is not a summary of what happened. It is a claim about what the novel shows, backed up with evidence from the text and your own explanation of what that evidence means. Here is the shape your essay takes, and which Quick Write each part grows from.
PART 1 · THESIS (from Quick Write 6)
Name your theme (loyalty, family, or staying gold) and state what the novel says about it. One clear claim the student will defend through the whole essay.
PART 2 · EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION, FIRST PIECE (from Quick Writes 3 and 4)
Point to a specific moment from your copy of the novel. Then explain in your own words what that moment shows about your theme. The explanation is the part that does the real work.
PART 3 · EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION, SECOND PIECE (from Quick Writes 4 and 5)
Give a second moment from the novel. Explain how it adds to your point about the theme. Two pieces of evidence build a stronger case than one.
PART 4 · CONCLUSION (from Quick Write 6)
Restate your point in fresh words and say what it tells us about the novel as a whole. Do not just repeat the thesis sentence.
📚 READ & NOTICE: A WORKED EXAMPLE

Here is a short literary analysis of a different theme in The Outsiders. Each labeled part matches the shape above. The example models the moves; it is not an answer to borrow.

THESIS In The Outsiders, the lives of the greasers and the Socs show that social class shapes how people see themselves and each other, and that neither group is free from that pressure.

EVIDENCE 1 Early in the novel, Ponyboy describes the greasers as constantly on guard in their own neighborhood. (Then the explanation:) This detail shows that class shapes daily life on a basic level: where you walk, how you feel safe, what you expect from strangers. Class is not just about money here; it is about belonging and threat.

EVIDENCE 2 Later, a Soc character admits to Ponyboy that the Socs have their own pressures and emptiness despite their advantages. (Then the explanation:) This complicates the theme: class divides the two groups, but it also traps both of them. The novel suggests that neither side can simply escape who they are in their world.

CONCLUSION By showing class from both sides, the novel argues that social division hurts everyone it touches, even those who appear to have the advantage.

Every piece of evidence is followed immediately by an explanation. The explanation answers: what does this specific moment show about the theme? That sentence is what earns credit, not the detail alone.

📚 CHECK YOUR OWN THINKING

Read this student sentence, which is meant to be the explanation after a piece of evidence:

“This shows that Johnny and Ponyboy are loyal to each other and are best friends in the novel.”
⚡ TRY IT · NAME EACH PART OF THE ESSAY

Below are sentences from a literary analysis essay, out of order. For each one, decide which part it is: thesis, evidence, explanation, or conclusion. Decide first, then choose.

1. “In the end, the novel argues that loyalty and love are what keep people human, even when the world around them makes that hard.”
2. “Near the end of the novel, one of the main characters leaves behind a written message for Ponyboy about what staying gold means.”
3. “This reveals that staying gold is not about being young forever but about holding onto something good even when life tries to take it from you.”
4. “Throughout The Outsiders, family, whether blood or found, is the one thing that gives the characters a reason to keep going.”
🧠 NOW BUILD YOUR PLAN

Fill in the four parts on paper or in a new document, using the chosen theme and original details:

Thesis: pull your claim from Quick Write 6. Make sure it names a theme (loyalty, family, or staying gold) and says what the novel shows about it.
Evidence 1 and its explanation: take your strongest detail from Quick Writes 3 or 4. Write one sentence that says what the detail shows about your theme. Cite it in MLA format: Hinton’s last name plus the chapter, for example (Hinton, Ch. 7). If you already name Hinton in your sentence, just give the chapter.
Evidence 2 and its explanation: add a second detail from Quick Writes 4 or 5. Explain it in the same way. Make sure the two pieces work together.
Conclusion: use your Quick Write 6 intro to restate the point in fresh words, then add one sentence about what it tells us about the novel overall.

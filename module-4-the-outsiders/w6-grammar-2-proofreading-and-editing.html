Grammar 2
Proofreading and Editing: Finding and Fixing Errors Before You Submit
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Proofreading is a skill, not just a chore. Today you learn to read your own writing with fresh eyes: catching the kinds of errors that hide when you are drafting fast. This matters right now because Quick Write 6 asks for a real essay outline and a draft introduction. Those need to be clean before you submit.

“He finally understood that the choices his friends made had affected him more then he could explain.”

💡 A PROOFREADING SYSTEM THAT WORKS
🔐
Pass 1: Spelling
Read slowly, word by word. Cover the next line with a sheet of paper so your eye does not skip ahead.
📋
Pass 2: Confused Words
Check every their/there/they're, its/it's, affect/effect, then/than. Ask the part-of-speech question.
📜
Pass 3: Sentence Boundaries
Check every sentence has a subject noun or subject pronoun and a verb. No fragments, no run-ons.
👁
Pass 4: Read Aloud
Whisper-read your draft. Your ear catches what your eye misses: awkward phrasing, missing words.
Four passes is not slow. Each pass takes 1 to 2 minutes for a short paragraph. Total: under 8 minutes. Worth it.
📚 LABELING THE CORRECTED SENTENCE

Here is the corrected sentence: "He finally understood that the choices his friends made had affected him more than he could explain."

  1. What is being said about he? He understood. The main verb is understood (action verb).
  2. Who or what understood? He. The answer is a subject pronoun.
  3. Understood what? That the choices had affected him. Had affected = helping verb (had) + main verb (affected).
  4. More than is a comparison marker. Than is a conjunction introducing the comparison clause.
  5. Finally tells when he understood: that is an adverb (answers: When?).
Key labels from the corrected sentence:
He
subject pronoun finally
adverb understood
verb choices
subject noun had
helping verb affected
main verb than
conjunction

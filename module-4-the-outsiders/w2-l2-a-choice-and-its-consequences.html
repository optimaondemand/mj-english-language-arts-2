A Choice and Its Consequences

🔊 READ ALOUD — LISTEN TO THIS PAGE
Today's Focus

Chapter 4 of The Outsiders contains one of the most important events in the whole novel. An already tense situation turns suddenly dangerous, and two characters make a decision in a moment of crisis. Today you will look carefully at what leads up to that moment, what happens, and how the characters respond. The question the chapter asks is real: when something terrible happens and you had a role in it, even if you were trying to protect someone, what do you do next?

By the End of This Lesson
  1. I will be able to describe the key events of Chapter 4 in my own words, without reproducing the author's text.
  2. I will be able to analyze why the characters made the choices they did, using details from the chapter.
  3. I will be able to form my own position on whether the decision at the end of the chapter was justified.

Part 2: Chapter 4 in Focus
~5 min

Read Chapter 4 in your copy. Then use the summary below to anchor your analysis.

What Happens in Chapter 4

Chapter 4 opens where Chapter 3 left off: Ponyboy is still out past curfew, and the tension between the greasers and the Socs has not resolved. A group of Socs confronts Ponyboy and Johnny in the park. The confrontation is not a conversation. It escalates quickly into a physical attack.

What happens next is the pivotal moment of the novel. Ponyboy is in direct physical danger. Johnny, acting to stop the attack and protect his friend, intervenes with force. The outcome of that intervention is irreversible: a member of the Socs dies. This is not a dramatic flourish in the novel -- it is handled with brevity and weight, because what matters to the author is not the violence itself but what comes after.

Ponyboy and Johnny face an immediate decision. They can stay and try to explain what happened, or they can run. They choose to run. They find Dally -- one of the older, harder greasers -- and ask for help. He gives them money, a weapon, and directions to a hiding place outside of town. By the time the chapter ends, the two boys are alone in an abandoned building, cut off from their neighborhood, their brothers, and the life they knew.

A Note on This Chapter

Chapter 4 contains a serious, violent event. The novel treats it with weight. As you analyze it, focus on why the characters make the choices they do, not just what happens. That is what the author wants you to think about.

Part 3: A Question of Justification
~5 min

When someone uses force to stop an attack against another person, the question of whether that force was justified is not always easy. Self-defense means acting to prevent harm to yourself or someone else when there is no other way to stop it. Whether Chapter 4 fits that description is something careful readers have debated. The novel does not settle the question for you. It puts the evidence in front of you and lets you decide.

TRY IT: WHICH SIDE DOES THIS EVIDENCE SUPPORT?
For each piece of evidence below, decide whether it supports a self-defense argument or raises a question about it.
Ponyboy was being physically attacked and held under water at the moment Johnny acted.
The characters chose to run rather than staying to explain what happened.
The attack was ongoing: the Socs had not stopped and showed no sign of stopping.
Johnny carried a weapon, which suggests he was prepared for a fight before that night.
Reading Response
  1. In your own words, describe what happens in Chapter 4 and why Johnny acted the way he did. What was at risk in that moment?
  2. Do you think Johnny's action was justified? Use at least one specific detail from the chapter to support your answer. You do not have to agree with what he did, but you do have to have a reason for your position.
  3. At the end of Chapter 4, Ponyboy and Johnny choose to run. What does that choice tell you about them? What are they afraid of?

The events of Chapter 4 change everything that comes after them. As you read, keep asking: what did each character think they had to do, and why? That question has no easy answer. That is exactly why Hinton wrote it.

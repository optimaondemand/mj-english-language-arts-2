Lesson 3.03 · Weekly Workshop
Theme and Evidence: Building a Paragraph About Staying Gold
Lesson ≈10 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Read Chapters 5–6 in your copy of The Outsiders (or finish reading if you have not yet). [Teacher: link the approved student copy / purchase or library access here]
Today you will learn what a theme statement is and how to support it with evidence. Then you will use that structure to write Quick Write 3, a theme-plus-evidence paragraph that goes directly into your module project file.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
State the difference between a topic and a theme statement.
2
Write a theme statement and support it with evidence from your own reading.
💡 TOPIC VS. THEME STATEMENT
Topic: a subject (not a statement)
“Lost innocence”
Names a subject. Does not make a claim. Cannot be right or wrong.
Theme statement: a claim about that subject
“The novel shows that once innocence is lost to violence and hardship, it cannot be recovered.”
Makes a claim. Can be supported with evidence. That is what a theme statement does.

A theme statement is not a fact about the plot. It is a claim about what the story argues. Good theme statements are general enough to apply to the whole novel, specific enough to be meaningful, and supportable with evidence from your copy of the text.

📚 THE PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE FOR QUICK WRITE 3

Here is the structure your Quick Write 3 paragraph should follow. Study the example, then write your own paragraph about the theme of “staying gold” or lost innocence in Chapters 5–6.

💡 PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE (FOUR PARTS)
In your Reading Journal, draft a one-sentence theme statement about Chapters 5–6, then use this structure for Quick Write 3.
  1. Theme statement (one sentence making a claim about what the novel argues). Example: In Chapters 5–6, the novel suggests that staying gold means holding on to wonder and honesty even when the world around you is violent and unfair.
  2. Evidence 1 (a detail from your reading, in your own words: what happens that supports the claim). Example: Ponyboy wakes before Johnny and watches the sunrise, noticing the colors and reciting the Frost poem, a moment of beauty in the middle of a crisis.
  3. Explanation (why that evidence supports the theme statement). Example: The fact that Ponyboy reaches for beauty and poetry even when he is frightened and in hiding shows that “gold” is not just a feeling; it is a choice to keep noticing what is good.
  4. Closing thought (one sentence connecting the evidence to the module’s larger question). Example: This moment sets up the question the rest of the novel asks: whether that choice is possible for someone who has seen what Ponyboy has seen.
Use this structure for your Quick Write 3. Your theme statement should be your own, not a copy of the example.

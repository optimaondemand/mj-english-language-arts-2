Grammar 2
Active and Passive Voice
Lesson
TODAY'S FOCUS
~12 min
A sentence in active voice has the subject doing the action. A sentence in passive voice has the subject receiving the action. Both are correct, but they do different things for a reader. Today you will learn to tell them apart and choose between them.
ACTIVE VS. PASSIVE VOICE
ACTIVE VOICE
The subject acts.
  • The subject noun performs the action.
  • The verb is a direct action verb.
  • Usually clearer and more direct.
EXAMPLE
"The boy threw the ball."
Subject (the boy) performs the action.
→
TRANSFORM
←
PASSIVE VOICE
The subject is acted on.
  • The subject receives the action.
  • The verb includes a helping verb (is, was, were, been, etc.) plus a past participle.
  • Can emphasize what happened rather than who did it.
EXAMPLE
"The ball was thrown by the boy."
Subject (the ball) receives the action.
WARM-UP
Which sounds more direct to you: "She solved the problem" or "The problem was solved by her"? Pick one and say why. (No wrong answer.)
👁 READ & NOTICE
CLASSIFYING THE TWO FOCUS SENTENCES
ACTIVE VOICE SENTENCE
"The team won the championship."

Step 1 What is being said about the team? The team won. Won is the verb (action verb).

Step 2 Who or what won? The team. Team is the subject noun.

Step 3 The team is doing the action. This is active voice.

Classified
The article adj.
team subject noun
won verb (action)
the article adj.
championship noun (direct obj.)
PASSIVE VOICE SENTENCE
"The championship was won by the team."

Step 1 What is being said about the championship? The championship was won. Was won is the verb (helping verb plus main verb).

Step 2 Who or what was won? The championship. Championship is the subject noun. Notice: it is not doing the winning; it is receiving the action.

Step 3 The team appears after "by" and is the agent. Championship is the subject noun. This is passive voice.

Classified
The article adj.
championship subject noun
was helping verb
won main verb
by preposition
the article adj.
team obj. of prep.
KEY RULE
To spot passive voice: look for a helping verb (was, were, is, are, been, being) paired with a past-participle main verb. If the doer of the action appears at all, it will usually follow "by."
📝 PRACTICE
ACTIVE OR PASSIVE?
Read each sentence. Decide whether it uses active or passive voice.
"The scientist discovered a new element." Active or passive voice?
"The new element was discovered by the scientist." Active or passive voice?
"Three workers repaired the bridge." Active or passive voice?
"The letter was delivered to the wrong address." Active or passive voice?
WHEN TO USE WHICH
  • Use active voice when you want to be direct and clear about who is doing what.
  • Use passive voice when what happened matters more than who did it, or when the doer is unknown.
  • In analytical writing about literature, active voice is usually the stronger choice.
✎️ MAKE & APPLY
In the Week 2 section of your Reading Journal, write two sentence pairs. For each pair, write the same idea first in active voice, then in passive voice. Then add one sentence saying which version is clearer and why.

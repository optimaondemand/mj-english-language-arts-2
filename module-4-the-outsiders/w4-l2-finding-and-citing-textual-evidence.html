Lesson 4.02
Finding and Citing Textual Evidence
Lesson ≈14 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
You will be able to find a passage in your own copy of The Outsiders that supports a claim about theme, quote it briefly and accurately, and explain in your own words what the passage shows.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you make a claim about a story, what do you usually use to back it up?
💡 THE THREE-PART EVIDENCE MOVE
1
Claim
State what you believe the text shows about a character or theme.
2
Quote
Use a short, exact piece of the text (a phrase or sentence) with quotation marks, the author’s last name, and the chapter number in parentheses.
3
Explain
In your own words, say what the quoted passage shows about your claim. Do not just repeat the quote.
📚 HOW IT WORKS: A MODELED EXAMPLE

When you write about theme in The Outsiders, your job is to show the reader exactly where in the text your idea comes from. A brief, precise quote does that. You do not need a long passage; one or two sentences from your copy of the book, placed between quotation marks, are enough. Here is what the three-part move looks like in practice (this example uses an original passage to show the structure, not text from the novel):

Modeled structure (original example sentence):

[Claim] Loyalty in the novel often shows up as physical presence, simply being there when someone needs you. [Quote] In one scene, the narrator notices that “nobody said a word” as they sat together waiting, and yet the silence itself felt like a kind of answer. (Hinton, Ch. 7) [Explain] This shows that the characters’ loyalty is not measured in speeches; it is measured in the willingness to stay.

Notice that the explanation does not just say “this quote shows loyalty.” It says how the quote shows loyalty. That is the move that makes an evidence paragraph convincing. Notice also that the citation names the author, Hinton, and the chapter. If you already name Hinton in your own sentence (Hinton writes that…), do not name her again in the parentheses; just give the chapter.

In your Reading Journal’s Week 4 section, find one place in Chapters 7 or 8 where a character shows loyalty or makes a small heroic choice. Write the three parts: state your claim, copy the passage (brief, in quotation marks, with the author’s last name and the chapter number), then explain it in your own words. This feeds directly into Quick Write 4.
📜 YOUR WORKS CITED ENTRY
The way you have just been citing has a name: MLA style. It is the style used for writing about literature, and it is what every essay in this course uses. Any essay that cites the novel, including your final essay, ends with one Works Cited entry for it:
Hinton, S. E. The Outsiders. 1967.

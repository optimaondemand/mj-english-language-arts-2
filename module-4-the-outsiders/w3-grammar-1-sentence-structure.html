Grammar 1
Sentence Structure: Simple, Compound, and Complex
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Good writers vary their sentence structure. A paragraph made up of only short, identical sentences sounds choppy. A paragraph made up of only long, tangled sentences is hard to follow. Today you will learn the three main structures, practice identifying them, and try building one of each.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCES (original examples, Module 4 context)
Simple: “The two boys sat in silence.”
Compound: “The two boys sat in silence, and the sun came up slowly over the hill.”
Complex: “Because they were hiding from everyone, the two boys had nothing to do but watch the sun rise.”
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you read the three example sentences above, which one felt most like the kind of sentence you write naturally? (No wrong answer.)
💡 THE THREE SENTENCE STRUCTURES
Simple
One independent clause. One complete subject and predicate. Nothing more is needed.

“The two boys sat in silence.”
Compound
Two independent clauses joined by a conjunction (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so). Each half could stand alone.

“The boys sat in silence, and the sun came up.”
Complex
One independent clause plus one dependent clause. The dependent clause cannot stand alone; it starts with a subordinating conjunction (because, when, although, since, after, if).

“Because they were hiding, the boys watched the sunrise.”
📚 HOW TO IDENTIFY THE STRUCTURE: ASK QUESTIONS ABOUT THE CLAUSES

Start with the compound sentence: “The two boys sat in silence, and the sun came up slowly over the hill.”

  1. What is being said about the two boys? They sat. So the subject noun is boys and the verb is sat. That is one complete clause.
  2. Is there another subject and verb after the conjunction and? Yes: sun came up. Subject noun: sun. Verb: came. That is a second complete clause.
  3. Two independent clauses joined by a conjunction: this is a compound sentence.

Now the complex sentence: “Because they were hiding from everyone, the two boys had nothing to do but watch the sun rise.”

  1. What is being said about the two boys in the main part? They had nothing to do. Subject noun: boys. Verb: had. That part can stand alone.
  2. Can “Because they were hiding from everyone” stand alone as a complete sentence? No. It depends on the main clause to make sense. It starts with the subordinating conjunction because, so it is a dependent clause.
  3. One independent clause plus one dependent clause: this is a complex sentence.

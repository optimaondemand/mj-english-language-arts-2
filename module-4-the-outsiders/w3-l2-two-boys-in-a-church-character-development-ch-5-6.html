Lesson 3.02
Two Boys in a Church: Character Development in Chapters 5–6
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Read Chapters 5–6 in your copy of The Outsiders. [Teacher: link the approved student copy / purchase or library access here]
The time Ponyboy and Johnny spend at the church hideout shows both characters changing. Today you will look at how a writer shows character development through actions, thoughts, and conversations, using Chapters 5–6 as your examples.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
Describe, in your own words, how Ponyboy changes during Chapters 5–6.
2
Describe, in your own words, how Johnny changes during Chapters 5–6.
3
Explain what this change shows about the novel’s central themes.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you think about who Johnny is before Chapters 5–6, how would you describe him? (No wrong answer.)
💡 WHAT CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT LOOKS LIKE ON THE PAGE
🕐
Actions
What a character does, especially under pressure, shows who they are becoming.
💬
Speech
What a character says (or chooses not to say) tells us about their feelings and values.
🧠
Thoughts
Because Ponyboy narrates, we see his inner thoughts directly. Watch what he notices and why.

In Chapters 5–6, the church functions as a place outside the gang world. Neither Ponyboy nor Johnny is a greaser or a Soc there. They are just two people. That matters for understanding who they become.

In your copy of the novel, look for moments where one of them says or does something that is different from who he was at the start of the book. You do not need to quote; think about the shape of the change.

📚 PONYBOY AND JOHNNY: WHAT CHANGES, AND HOW

Ponyboy at the hideout. Ponyboy arrives frightened and disoriented. During the days in hiding, he reads, talks, watches sunrises, and thinks about his family. He misses home. He reaches for beauty, for the poem, for the sky. His inner world is on display in a way that the streets of his neighborhood never allow. By the time Dally arrives, Ponyboy is not the same boy who fled. He has started asking larger questions about what he is, what his life means, and what “gold” feels like before it is gone.

Johnny at the hideout. Johnny arrives carrying guilt and fear. But something steadies him in the church. He takes charge of practical matters, cuts Ponyboy’s hair, makes decisions about what to do next. He seems calmer, more decisive than he was on the streets. The person who was the most beaten down in the gang shows, under the pressure of hiding, that he has more resolve than anyone expected, including himself.

⚡ TRY IT · CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT
Based on what you read and the description above, choose the best answer for each question.
1. What does Ponyboy’s behavior at the church most reveal about his character?
2. What is most surprising about Johnny’s behavior in Chapters 5–6?
3. What does the church setting suggest about how people develop?

Grammar 2
Punctuating Titles and Quotations
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
You will be able to punctuate titles of long works and short works correctly, and format a quoted passage from a book with the right punctuation. This skill goes directly into Quick Write 4 and the module project.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you write the title of a book in your notes or an essay, how do you usually handle it?
💡 THE ONE RULE TO REMEMBER: SHORT GETS QUOTES, LONG GETS ITALICS
Short works: quotation marks
  • Short stories: “The Most Dangerous Game”
  • Poems: “Nothing Gold Can Stay”
  • Song titles: “Greaser Blues”
  • Single episodes of a TV show
  • Articles and chapters
Long works: italics (or underline in handwriting)
  • Novels: The Outsiders
  • Films: Rumble Fish
  • Albums: Abbey Road
  • Newspapers, magazines
  • Plays, musicals
📚 QUOTING FROM A BOOK: THE RULES

When you copy exact words from a book into your essay or journal, you place them inside quotation marks. The period or comma at the end goes inside the closing quotation mark in American English. The chapter or page citation goes in parentheses before the final period.

📚 PRACTICE SENTENCE
“Marcus looked out the window and said quietly, ‘I’m not leaving him here.’”
  1. What is being said about Marcus? He looked and said. The verbs are looked and said (both action verbs).
  2. Who or what looked and said? Marcus. Marcus is the subject noun (proper noun, capitalized).
  3. Where does the period go in a quoted sentence? Inside the closing quotation mark: “...leaving him here.”
  4. What about a quote within a quote? The outer speaker’s words get double quotation marks (“ ”); the character’s spoken words inside that get single quotation marks (‘ ’).
Classified sentence:
Marcus
subject noun (proper) looked
verb (action) out
preposition the
article adjective window
noun (obj. of prep.) and
conjunction said
verb (action) quietly
adverb (How?) ,“...”
quotation punctuation

One more rule: when you introduce a quotation with a signal phrase like “he said” or “the narrator explains,” use a comma after the signal phrase before opening the quotation marks. Example: The narrator observes, “Nobody was laughing now.” (Ch. 7)

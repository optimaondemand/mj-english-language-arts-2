LESSON 4.06

Reading Your Way to a Claim

Weekly Workshop
~10 min
TODAY'S FOCUS

This is your weekly workshop lesson. It is shorter than the others. The goal is to pull together what you have read and practiced this week and put it into one analytical sentence. That sentence is your Quick Write 2 claim: a statement about a character's choice in Chapters 3 or 4 that you can support with details from the novel.

BY THE END OF THIS LESSON
  1. I will be able to state an original claim about a character's choice in Chapters 3-4.
  2. I will be able to identify at least one piece of support for that claim from the chapters.
MATERIALS
  • Your Outsiders Reading Journal (with your Week 2 notes).
  • Your copy of The Outsiders, Chapters 3-4.
  • Microsoft Word for your Quick Write.

What Makes a Claim?
Not a Claim

In Chapter 4, something bad happens and Johnny and Ponyboy run away.

This just retells events.
→ Add your interpretation. →
A Claim

Johnny's decision in Chapter 4, though drastic, was driven by loyalty rather than anger.

This takes a position that needs support.
A claim does not just describe what happened. It says what you think it means.
PART 1: READ & NOTICE
~2 min

Before you build your claim, think about which character's choices have been on your mind this week.

WARM-UP
Which character's choice from Chapters 3 or 4 are you most curious about? (Pick one to start thinking.)
PART 2: BUILDING YOUR CLAIM
~6 min

A claim is a sentence that says what you think and that someone could argue against. If no one could disagree, it is not a claim. It is just a fact. Read the examples below. Each card shows a starting point on the front. Click the card to see a stronger version of the same idea as a full claim.

STARTING POINT: Click to see a stronger claim.
Johnny made a choice in Chapter 4.
STRONGER CLAIM
Johnny acted to protect someone he cared about, which shows that loyalty is a more powerful force in this novel than law or consequence.
STARTING POINT: Click to see a stronger claim.
Ponyboy reacted to the events of Chapter 3.
STRONGER CLAIM
Ponyboy's reaction to Cherry at the movies shows that the divide between greasers and Socs is more about economics than about who people actually are inside.
STARTING POINT: Click to see a stronger claim.
Dally gave Ponyboy and Johnny advice after the events of Chapter 4.
STRONGER CLAIM
Dally's advice to run rather than face the consequences reveals that the greasers have learned to distrust a justice system that was never designed to protect them.

These are examples only. Your Quick Write 2 claim should be your own.

Reading Journal: Week 2 Section

Before you start Quick Write 2, write two sentences in your journal: your working claim about a character's choice, and one detail from Chapters 3 or 4 that you think supports it.

WHAT YOU'LL DO NEXT
  1. Quick Write 2 (assignment): Write your claim about a character's choice. Use the M365 Word doc and save it to your OneDrive.
  2. Check-up (quiz): Covers this week's reading and grammar. Then confirm your submissions.
BEFORE YOU GO

A strong analytical sentence does more work than a summary. It says what you think the evidence means. This week you practiced noticing signals, weighing a difficult decision, and now building a position. That is the whole arc of reading like an analyst.

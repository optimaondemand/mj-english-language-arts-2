Grammar 1
Commonly Confused Words: Getting the Right Word in the Right Place
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
Some words sound alike or look similar but do very different jobs in a sentence. Choosing the wrong one is one of the most common errors in student writing. Today you will nail down the five most important pairs, including the ones that trip up writers every day.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (original, Ch. 11 context)
“Their loyalty was something he could feel but not easily describe in words.”
(Original sentence, not from the novel text. Used here to practice this week's grammar.)
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before this lesson, how often do you stop to double-check words like their/there/they're or affect/effect when you write? (No wrong answer.)
💡 THE FIVE PAIRS TO KNOW
Word What it does Anchor sentence
their possessive pronoun: belongs to them The greasers kept their loyalty.
there location or sentence starter He stood there alone.
they're contraction of they are They're not so different.
affect verb: to influence or change The loss affected Ponyboy deeply.
effect noun: the result or outcome The effect was lasting.
its possessive: belonging to it The gang had its own code.
it's contraction of it is It's not easy to explain.
then time: after that He read the letter, then he cried.
than comparison: used to compare He was older than he seemed.
Quick test for contractions: if you can say the full two words, use the contraction. If not, use the possessive. ("It is easy" works, so write it's. "Belong to it" works, so write its.)
📚 USING THE SENTENCE TO CHECK WORD CHOICE

Look at this week's sentence: "Their loyalty was something he could feel but not easily describe in words."

  1. What is being said about their loyalty? It was something. The verb is was, a linking verb.
  2. Who or what was? Loyalty. That is the subject noun.
  3. Their sits before loyalty. Can we replace it with "they are loyalty"? No. So it is the possessive: their, not they're.
  4. Describe is a verb (action verb). What about the effect of loyalty? That would be a noun. What about whether loss affected him? That would be a verb. Notice how the part of speech tells you which word to pick.

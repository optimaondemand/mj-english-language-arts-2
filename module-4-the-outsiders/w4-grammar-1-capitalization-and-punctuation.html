Grammar 1
Capitalization and Punctuation
Lesson ≈12 min
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
You will be able to apply the core rules of capitalization and end punctuation, and identify how they work inside a classified sentence.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you proofread your own writing, which rule do you find hardest to remember?
💡 THE CORE RULES AT A GLANCE
Capitalize
  • The first word of every sentence
  • The word I (always)
  • Proper nouns: names of specific people, places, organizations, days, months
  • Titles used directly before a name (Officer Hardy, Dr. Thomas)
Do NOT capitalize
  • Common nouns used generally (the doctor, a street)
  • Seasons: spring, summer, fall, winter
  • Titles used alone or after a name (the officer; James Hardy, an officer)
End punctuation: match it to the sentence’s purpose
  • Statement (declarative): period.
  • Question (interrogative): question mark?
  • Strong feeling (exclamatory): exclamation mark!
  • Command or request (imperative): period. (sometimes !)
📚 ANALYZING A SENTENCE
📚 THIS WEEK’S PRACTICE SENTENCE
“Marcus, a greaser from Tulsa, stood quietly outside the hospital on a cold Tuesday night.”
  1. What is being said about Marcus? He stood. The verb is stood (action verb).
  2. Who or what stood? Marcus. Marcus is the subject noun.
  3. What kind of subject noun is Marcus? It is a proper noun: a specific person’s name. Capitalized.
  4. What other proper noun appears? Tulsa: the name of a specific city. Capitalized. Tuesday: the name of a specific day. Capitalized.
  5. What end mark does this sentence use? A period, because it is a declarative sentence (a statement).
Classified sentence:
Marcus
subject noun (proper) ,
punctuation a
article adjective greaser
noun from
preposition Tulsa
proper noun (obj. of prep.) , stood
verb (action) quietly
adverb (How?) outside
preposition the
article adjective hospital
noun (obj. of prep.) on a cold
prep. + art. adj. + adj. Tuesday
proper noun (adj. use) night.
noun (obj. of prep.) + period

Notice: greaser is a common noun (lowercase) even though it names a group. Tulsa and Tuesday are proper nouns (capitalized) because they name a specific place and a specific day. The sentence ends with a period because it is a statement.

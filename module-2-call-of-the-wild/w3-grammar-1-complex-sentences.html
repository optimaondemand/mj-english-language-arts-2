Complex Sentences: Joining an Idea That Cannot Stand Alone
Lesson
📝 TODAY'S FOCUS
⌚ ~12 min
A complex sentence joins two ideas that are not equal: one can stand on its own, and one cannot. Today you will see how London uses this to show that one thing depends on another, the way Buck’s anger depends on what Spitz just did.
📚 THIS WEEK'S SENTENCE (from Chapter 3)
“When Buck finished his ration and returned, he found his nest occupied.”
The moment Buck discovers Spitz has stolen his warm sleeping spot at Lake Le Barge.
⚡ WARM-UP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Read just the opening part: “When Buck finished his ration and returned.” Does that group of words feel like a finished thought you could say and stop? (No wrong answer.)
💡 THE ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. There are two kinds. A complex sentence joins one of each.
✅
Independent clause
A complete thought. It can stand alone as a sentence.
“he found his nest occupied”
⏬
Dependent clause
Has a subject and verb, but cannot stand alone. A starter word makes it lean on the rest.
“When Buck finished his ration and returned”
The little word at the front of a dependent clause is its conjunction. Words like when, because, after, since, although, if, and while are the most common ones. They are the signal that the idea cannot stand on its own.
📚 HOW TO READ A COMPLEX SENTENCE

Find the two clauses, then test each one alone. Watch how it works on this week’s sentence.

  1. Split it at the comma. Part one: “When Buck finished his ration and returned.” Part two: “he found his nest occupied.”
  2. Test part two alone. “He found his nest occupied” is a complete thought. It can stand by itself. That makes it the independent clause.
  3. Test part one alone. “When Buck finished his ration and returned” leaves you waiting. The conjunction when makes it lean on part two. That makes it the dependent clause.
  4. Name it. One independent clause plus one dependent clause equals a complex sentence.
Label what you found:
When Buck finished his ration and returned
dependent clause , he found his nest occupied
independent clause

When the dependent clause comes first, a comma separates the two parts. London puts the “when” up front so you feel the wait, then drops the bad news: the nest is taken.
